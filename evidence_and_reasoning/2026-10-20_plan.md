## 0. The finding this plan builds on

Ledger claims 019 and 021 (prompts 287 to 290; verified exactly, three
independent passes at prompt 308).  Let $\mathcal O := \tfrac12 (e_1 + e_2) L$.
Then:

- $\mathcal O$ is a maximal order of integral octonions for the paper's
  multiplication (2.1): it contains $e_0$, is closed, and its trace form is
  unimodular.  It is one of the seven orders containing all eight basis
  units; in Wilson's notation it is his ring $A_3 = \tfrac12 (1 + i_3) B (1 +
  i_3)$ with $B = \tfrac12 L\bar s$, and Wilson's multiplication table is (2.1)
  under $i_t \mapsto e_{t+1}$ (checked at prompt 308).
- $L = (e_1 + e_2)\,\mathcal O$ (immediate from the definition, since
  $(e_1 + e_2)^2 = -2$ and $L = -L$), $\sigma(L\bar s) = 2\mathcal O$, and
  $\sigma(Ls) = p\,\mathcal O$ with $p = e_2 - e_4 + e_5 - e_6$.  Each of the
  last two is eight products (a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathcal O$) plus the
  covolume count $\operatorname{covol}(x\mathcal O) = N(x)^4
  \operatorname{covol}(\mathcal O) = N(x)^4/16$.  The multipliers $e_1 + e_2$
  and $p$ are purely imaginary elements of $\mathcal O$.  Right multiples do
  not work ($\mathcal O (e_1+e_2) \not\subseteq L$, $\mathcal O p \not\subseteq
  \sigma(Ls)$).
- For purely imaginary $x$ and all octonions $a, b$: $(xa)(xb) = x((ax)b)$.
  Four lines from the left Moufang identity, flexibility and $x^2 = -N(x)$;
  the defect for general $x$ is $2\operatorname{Re}(x)\,[x, a, b]$.
- Hence $(x\mathcal O)(x\mathcal O) \subseteq x((\mathcal O x)\mathcal O)
  \subseteq x\mathcal O$: Lemma 4.2 ($x = e_1 + e_2$), Lemma 4.4 ($x = p$);
  Lemma 4.3 is $L \cdot 2\mathcal O \subseteq 2\mathcal O$ from $L \subseteq
  \mathcal O$.

The three 64-entry tables of Appendix A thus reduce to one classical fact
(closure of a maximal order, citable; also 64 products), two eight-product
lattice identities, and four lines of algebra.  Lemma 4.1 never needed a
table.

## 2. The symbol for the maximal order (decision (a))

The working symbol $O$ clashes with $0$ and with $\mathbb O$.  Considered:

- $\mathcal O$ (script O, `\mathcal{O}`): the standard symbol for an order
  or a ring of integers in algebraic number theory and in the arithmetic of
  quaternion and octonion algebras ($\mathcal O_K$; orders $\mathcal O$ in
  Vignéras and Voight).  Distinct from $\mathbb O$ and from $0$ in print.
  Formulas read $L = (e_1 + e_2)\mathcal O$, $\sigma(L\bar s) = 2\mathcal O$,
  $\sigma(Ls) = p\mathcal O$, $\mathcal O \cdot \mathcal O \subseteq
  \mathcal O$.
- $\mathbb L$ (the author's suggestion): the paper reserves blackboard
  letters for number systems and algebras ($\mathbb R, \mathbb Z, \mathbb Q,
  \mathbb C, \mathbb H, \mathbb O, \mathbb F$); and $L = (e_1 + e_2)\mathbb L$
  sets $L$ and $\mathbb L$ side by side, one stroke apart.
- $\mathcal L$: no precedent for an order; the same $L$-proximity.
- The sources' own letters: Coxeter and Kirmse write $J$ (and $J_1$ for
  Kirmse's set), Wilson writes $A$ (and $B$ for $\tfrac12 L\bar s$).  $J$ is
  tied to Kirmse's set in Appendix B; $A$ is a generic lattice name in
  Section 4's preamble.  Conway and Smith's symbol for the octavians could
  not be checked (the book is not in the repository).

Recommendation: $\mathcal O$, with a macro `\newcommand{\MO}{\mathcal{O}}`
next to `\OO`.  A build with $\mathbb L$ for comparison costs one line; the
author decides on sight.
