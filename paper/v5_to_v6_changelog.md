### 1.2 The automorphism group, settled (§5.3, Remark 5.5)

- **What changed.** v5 recorded only the empirical bound $S_3 \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda,+,\star) \subsetneq \mathrm{Co}_0$ and posed full identification as open. It is now determined: $\mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda,+,\star)$ is **finite of order 36, with structure $C_6 \times S_3$**. The $S_3$ is the block permutation of Definition 3.3; the $C_6$ is generated by the signed permutation of imaginary units $e_1 \mapsto -e_5$, $e_2 \mapsto -e_7$, $e_3 \mapsto e_2$, $e_4 \mapsto -e_4$, $e_5 \mapsto e_6$, $e_6 \mapsto e_1$, $e_7 \mapsto -e_3$ fixing $e_0$. Every automorphism is a blockwise octonion automorphism followed by a block permutation.

  The remark also settles the ambient question first: $\mathbb{R}^{24} = \mathbb{O}^3$ splits as $D \oplus T$ with $D = \{(a,a,a) : a \in \mathbb{O}\}$ (dimension 8) and $T = \{(p,q,r) : p+q+r = 0\}$ (dimension 16) mutually annihilating two-sided ideals, whence $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^{24}, \star) = G_2 \times G_2 \times S_3$. The containment $\mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda,+,\star) \subsetneq \mathrm{Co}_0$ is thereby **proved, not asserted**: a real octonion automorphism preserves the positive-definite norm and $D \perp T$, so every $\star$-automorphism of $\mathbb{R}^{24}$ is orthogonal; strictness follows because $-I_{24} \in \mathrm{Co}_0$ does not preserve $\star$. Completeness of the order-36 claim rests on one non-enumerative step, the classification of the automorphisms of the complexification of $(T,\star)$; the lower bound $C_6 \times S_3$ and the strictness witness do not depend on it, and the remark says so.
- **Where.** New §5.3; the §7 automorphism bullet (§2 below); the abstract and §1 are unchanged.
- **Rationale.** Review finding R3 objected that v5 asserted the $\mathrm{Co}_0$ containment without proof. Rather than retreat to a weaker phrasing, the containment was proved, and the group computed. Scripts: `verify_aut_lambda_star.py`, `verify_aut_octonion_crosscheck.py`, `verify_ideal_decomposition.py`, `verify_star_algebra_structure.py` (in `python_project/src/`), and `aut_lambda_star.g`, `octonion_stabilisers.g` with their generator files (in `gap_project/`). The derivation is written out in full in the standalone note (§5 below).

### 1.3 Idempotents, classified (§5.2, Remark 5.3)

- **What changed.** New remark. The ambient algebra $(\mathbb{R}^{24}, +, \star)$ has **exactly eight** idempotents: $0$; $\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \varepsilon_3$ (the block units $(e_0,0,0)$ etc.); $\tfrac13\omega$ with $\omega = \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 + \varepsilon_3$; and $\varepsilon_i - \tfrac13\omega$. Every block of each is a multiple of $e_0$: no imaginary part, no positive-dimensional family. **None lies in Λ** (because $e_0 \notin L$), so the order has no idempotent. The passage from algebra to lattice is a rescaling: the least positive lattice multiples are $4\varepsilon_i$ ($N = 16$), $6(\varepsilon_i - \tfrac13\omega)$ ($N = 24$) and $4\omega$ ($N = 48$), each satisfying $u \star u = n\,u$ for its own $n$; and the rescaling is already visible on the minimal shell, where for each of the 84 purely imaginary roots λ of $L$ the minimal vector $(2\lambda,0,0)$ squares to $-8\varepsilon_1 \in \Lambda$.
- **Where.** New §5.2, first remark.
- **Rationale.** v5 could say only that no *minimal* vector is idempotent. The classification settles the question on all of Λ and on the ambient algebra. Scripts: `verify_idempotent_classification.py`, `verify_idempotent_lattice_rescaling.py`.

### 1.4 Square-zero elements: a v5 claim rescoped (§5.2, Remark 5.4)

- **What changed.** New remark, and it corrects the reading of a v5 statement. In the ambient algebra, $u = (x,y,z)$ satisfies $u \star u = 0$ exactly when $x, y, z$ are purely imaginary, of equal norm, and sum to zero (a hexagonal triple in $\mathrm{Im}(\mathbb{O}) = \mathbb{R}^7$): a 12-dimensional cone inside $T$, meeting $D$ only in 0. **Λ does contain nonzero square-zero elements**: 4,032 of them, all of norm 12, from the hexagonal triples of the $E_7$ root system formed by the 126 norm-4 vectors of $L\bar{s} \cap \mathrm{Im}(\mathbb{O})$. The exhaustive minimal-shell search reported in v5 could not have found them, for an arithmetic reason now stated: $N(u) = 3N(x)$ with $N(x) \in 2\mathbb{Z}$ and every Λ-norm in $4\mathbb{Z}$, so every square-zero vector of Λ has norm in $12\mathbb{Z}$, while the minimal norm is 8.
- **Where.** New §5.2, second remark. The deleted v5 remark's surviving content ("neither 0 nor 8 occurs among the product norms, so no minimal vector is idempotent and none squares to zero") is now one sentence of Remark 5.1, which points forward to §5.2 for the unrestricted answer.
- **Rationale.** The v5 sentence "the minimal shell contains no idempotents and no square nilpotents" was true as written but invited the unscoped reading "Λ has no nilpotents", which is **false**. The scoped statement is the only one the paper now makes. Script: `verify_square_zero_classification.py`.

### 1.5 Which twists close: the cycle census (§5.5, Remark 5.7)

- **What changed.** New remark with an exhaustive table. Writing $x \cdot_\pi y := \pi(\pi(x) \cdot \pi(y))$ and testing closure exactly on the 576 basis-pair products of a ℤ-basis of Λ, every cycle type of $S_7$ has been enumerated: transpositions 21/21 (100%), 3-cycles 35/70 (50%), 4-cycles 0/210 (0%), 5-cycles 252/504 (50%), 6-cycles 210/840 (25%), 7-cycles 336/720 (46.7%); total 854/2,365 (36.1%). The counts are exhaustive, not sampled. The 4-cycles fail without exception; only the 3-cycle half is explained (Remark 4.9).
- **Where.** New §5.5. Remark 4.9 (consecutive twists, with the 42/105 count for $(2,2)$-double transpositions) keeps its content, takes dash-sweep edits only, and is now cross-referenced from the new remark.
- **Rationale.** Review finding (recommended 8): the §7 bullet asking about other permutations was speculative while an exact enumeration was already available in the repository. Scripts: `verify_all_cycles_exact.py` (3- through 7-cycles), `consistency_checks.py` (transpositions).

## 2. Section 7 (Conclusion): bullets rewritten

- **What changed.** The opening paragraph drops "remarkably simple" and replaces the undefined "the Coxeter representation" with "Wilson's sublattice representation". Four of the five open-question bullets were rewritten; the fifth is unchanged:
  1. *A structural reason for Lemma 4.4.* Now records a **candidate explanation that has been excluded**: σ(*Ls*) is not an ideal of $L$ on either side. Of the 64 basis-pair products, 32 of $L \cdot \sigma(Ls)$ and 43 of $\sigma(Ls) \cdot L$ lie outside σ(*Ls*). By contrast σ(*L*$\bar{s}$) **is** a two-sided ideal of $L$, of which Lemma 4.3 is the left-handed half, and that asymmetry survives reduction mod $2L$ ($V$ an ideal, $W$ only a subalgebra). The v5 text had offered "a left ideal" as one of the shapes a structural reason might take; that shape is now ruled out.
  2. *The automorphism group.* Was "full identification is open". Now: where $\mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda,+,\star) \cong C_6 \times S_3$ sits inside $\mathrm{Co}_0$ up to conjugacy, and whether the order-6 octonion automorphism generating the $C_6$ admits an intrinsic description.
  3. *Maximality.* Was "whether the order is maximal in any appropriate sense". Now stated as a lattice question with a finite attack: is there a $\Lambda' \supsetneq \Lambda$ of finite index with $\Lambda' \star \Lambda' \subseteq \Lambda'$? Any such $\Lambda'$ lies in $\tfrac1n\Lambda$, so for each $n$ this is a finite check of the kind already used.
  4. *Classification of $(\mathbb{R}^{24},+,\star)$.* Unchanged.
  5. *Which twists close.* Replaces the old bullet asking whether other linear coordinate permutations of $\mathbb{R}^8$ conjugate the octonion product into one that closes: the enumeration is complete (Remark 5.7), so the residue is a structural characterization: a reason for the total failure of the 4-cycles and the exact one-half rates, the permutation types not yet enumerated, and linear maps of $\mathbb{R}^8$ that are not coordinate permutations.
- **Where.** §7.
- **Rationale.** Review findings R6 and recommended 7, 8, 9, 10, 19. Script for the ideal exclusion: `verify_sigma_Ls_ideal_exclusion.py`.

## 4. Clarity and precision (review pass, 10 July 2026)

A journal-grade review of v6 against an applied-audience lens is recorded at [paper/reviews/2026-07-10_claude_fable_5_journal_review_v6.md](reviews/2026-07-10_claude_fable_5_journal_review_v6.md): 6 required findings plus recommendations and notes. R2, R3, R5 and R6 are covered in §§1–3 above. The remainder:

- **σ = (1 2) is now fixed in the main text (R1).** The main text quantified over a generic transposition σ = (*i j*) while the appendix certificates cover only σ = (1 2). §4's preamble now fixes σ = (1 2) without loss of generality, with a footnote recording that the 21 transpositions of imaginary basis units form a single orbit under the Fano-plane automorphism group $\mathrm{GL}(3,\mathbb{F}_2)$ and that each of the 21 is verified to close (`consistency_checks.py`). That footnote is what grounds the generic statement of Theorem 1.1.
- **The §4 preamble no longer says σ "leaves the octonions invariant" (recommended 15).** σ is a transposition of two imaginary basis units; it is *not* an octonion automorphism. What it does is fix $L$ setwise (Lemma 4.1) and carry the octonion product to an isomorphic one (Definition 3.2), while *moving* Wilson's sublattices $L\bar{s}$ and $Ls$. The preamble now says exactly that, and names Lemmas 4.3 and 4.4 as where the work of the proof lives.
- **"The octonion algebra over $\mathbb{F}_2$" renamed (R4).** $L/2L$ is not an octonion algebra in the composition-algebra sense (those are simple; this one has the proper two-sided ideal $V$). Appendix A.1 now calls it "the reduction of the octonion order $L$ modulo 2, an eight-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_2$-algebra", with a footnote on the non-simplicity, and uses that name consistently. The non-commutativity footnote, which had framed the difference as one of *basis*, is recast as a difference of *order*: reducing the Gravesian order $\mathbb{Z}\langle e_0,\ldots,e_7\rangle$ gives a commutative $\mathbb{F}_2$-algebra, whereas the maximal order $L$ contains half-integer elements such as $s$, for which the commutator need not lie in $2L$.
- **Abstract glosses "order" (recommended 2):** "…admits the structure of an order in a 24-dimensional real algebra: the lattice is closed under a bilinear product on $\mathbb{R}^{24}$", forestalling the ordering-relation misreading.
- **§1 seams repaired (recommended 14, 16, 18).** The triple-product paragraph and the twist paragraph were rewritten as plain statements; the scope paragraph names the index $2^{16}$ instead of "exactly computed index"; Remark 1.3 no longer repeats the full product-norm value set, keeping only the takeaway (norm ≥ 16, strictly higher shells) and pointing to Remark 5.1.
- **Section 5 property table names its script (recommended 1):** `verify_section5_properties.py`, the last computational claim in the paper without a named script.

## 7. The span-defect clarity revision (the original v6 scope)

These three changes were the whole of v6 as of 10 July, before the review and the new results.

### Change 1 — §6 φ span comparison: restructured

- **What changed.** The single dash-linked sentence comparing the span defects of $\star$ and $\varphi$ was split into three shorter sentences (dash construction removed). No clarifying footnote is attached: the possible misreading of the comparison (as if the Baez–Egan construction failed to close) is instead resolved at the source, by the index explanation added to the span remark (Change 3) together with the anatomy paragraph's own opening, which already states that the Baez–Egan closure lives on the 27-dimensional ambient lattice and that $\varphi$ is the projection read-off.
- **Where.** §6, end of the "Anatomy of the Baez–Egan closure" paragraph.
- **Rationale.** As written in v5, the comparison could be misread as claiming the Baez–Egan construction fails to close; the fix grounds the reader in what a span defect is before the comparison arrives.

### Change 2 — (folded into Changes 1 and 3)

An intermediate revision on 10 July added a complementarity sentence to the span remark and a clarifying footnote to §6; both were superseded within the v6 cycle by the sharper index explanation of Change 3 and removed as redundant. Neither appears in v6.

### Change 3 — the index explained (now Remark 5.6)

- **What changed.** The redundant closure-versus-reach sentence (added earlier the same day) was removed. In its place, after the computed index, a plain explanation of what the index means: the index counts the cosets of $S$ in Λ; Λ decomposes into $[\Lambda : S]$ disjoint translates of $S$; every product and every integer combination of products lies in $S$ alone; a span-surjective product would have index 1; here $2^{16} - 1$ of every $2^{16}$ cosets are unreachable.
- **Where.** §5, Remark 5.4 in the v5 numbering; **Remark 5.6** after the restructure of §1.1.
- **Rationale.** "Index" was used without definition; the surjective baseline (index 1) makes the size of the defect legible to a non-specialist.
