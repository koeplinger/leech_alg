Prompt 120 -- Companion: why "sigma fixes L" and "sigma moves J_1" do not conflict
Date: 2026-05-22

User: "Ok. One gap in our understanding: I always understood sigma as a
placeholder that permits the concrete example of flipping axes (1, 2) but
that's just the working concrete example (just like the representation of
Coxeter's octonion multiplication is a concrete working example).

You write: 'sigma fixes L (sigma(L)=L, because D_8^+ is symmetric under every
coordinate permutation).' but before that you also wrote 'Kirmse's table .K
together with Kirmse's lattice J_1. The transposition sigma=(1 2) repairs
that pair: sigma(J_1) is closed under .K.' -- why is this not a contradiction?"

Answer: not a contradiction -- sigma(L)=L and sigma(J_1)!=J_1 are statements
about two DIFFERENT lattices. A coordinate transposition is one fixed map on
R^8; whether it fixes a given lattice X depends on X's own symmetry. L=D_8^+
and J_1 are both E8-type lattices but PLACED differently in R^8 relative to
the coordinate axes: L is the maximally symmetric placement (invariant under
every coordinate permutation); J_1 is a placement invariant under none of the
21 imaginary-axis transpositions. "Being E8" fixes the isometry type, not the
placement; only the placement decides coordinate symmetry. sigma fixing L and
moving J_1 is not a paradox -- it is the mechanism: sigma must fix the lattice
that stays closed (L) and move the lattices whose closure is redirected
(J_1 for Coxeter, Ls for us).

(verify_coxeter_1946.py shows all 21 transpositions send J_1 to domains
distinct from J_1); D_8^+ invariant under all coordinate permutations.
