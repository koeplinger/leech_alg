\begin{abstract}
This note accompanies the Verification and Commentary of 16 April
2026 on the Baez--Egan embedding
(\texttt{paper/Baez-Egan\_Leech\_Verification.pdf}, 8\,pp).  It sets
out an explicit lattice-algebraic parallel between Baez/Egan's ``key
lemma'' (Part 9, $L_1 \cap L_2 = 2 L_0$) and Wilson's identity
($L\bar s \cap Ls = 2 L$), and how Appendix~A of our paper
\emph{An order on the Leech lattice from a $\ZZ_3$-symmetric
triple-octonion product} (\texttt{paper/main.tex}, v8), ``The closure
criterion modulo~$2$'', builds on that shared identity.  It credits
the shared lattice-algebraic foundation and the prior work on $L/2L$,
and states what our paper adds.
\end{abstract}

% ===================================================================
\section{The shared key lemma}
% ===================================================================

Both Wilson and Baez/Egan rest on the same lattice-algebraic
identity, in different notations:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l@{\hskip 2em}l}
Baez/Egan (Part 9; \S4 of the Verification and Commentary): &
$L_1 \cap L_2 = 2 L_0$, \\[2pt]
Wilson (2009, Section~2): &
$L\bar s \cap Ls = 2 L$. \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In Baez/Egan's setup, $L_0 \cong E_8$ is self-dual; $L_1, L_2 \cong
\sqrt{2}\,E_8$ are sublattices of~$L_0$ arising from a common
rotation~$R$, and the duality argument $\mathrm{dual}(L_1 \cap L_2) =
\tfrac12 L_1 + \tfrac12 L_2 = \tfrac12 L_0$ gives
$L_1 \cap L_2 = 2 L_0$ (Egan).

In Wilson's setup, $L = D_8^+$ is the octonion-realized $E_8$; the
sublattices~$L\bar s$ and~$Ls$ are the images of~$L$ under right
multiplication by $\bar s$ and~$s$ respectively (so each is isomorphic to
$\sqrt{2}\,E_8$, since $\Nrm(s) = \Nrm(\bar s) = 2$), and Wilson
states the identity directly.

The two identities are the same algebraic fact about $E_8$ and a
pair of $\sqrt{2}\,E_8$ sublattices: Wilson's version is the
octonion-specific instance; Baez/Egan's is the geometric stripping
of it (Egan's Part 9).  Either form was already in the literature
before this project began.

\section{What Baez/Egan use it for, and what they did not do}
% ===================================================================

Baez/Egan use the key lemma in Part~9 as a step in establishing
that the lattice
\[
  L_L \;=\; \{(a, b, c) \in L_0^3 :
    a, b, c \in L_0;\;
    a{+}b, a{+}c, b{+}c \in L_1;\;
    a{+}b{+}c \in L_2\}
\]
is isometric to $\sqrt{2}\,\LL$.  The identity $L_1 \cap L_2 = 2 L_0$
is used in showing that $L_L/\sqrt{2}$ is (i)~self-dual and
(ii)~without vectors of squared norm~$2$.  This is the use in Egan's
Part~9 of the original $n$-Category Caf\'e series.

Baez/Egan do \emph{not}, anywhere in the Part~9 / Part~10 sequence,
descend the octonion product on $L_0 = E_8$ modulo~$2 L_0$ to read
$L_0 / 2 L_0$ as an $\FF_2$-bilinear algebra and analyze the sublattice
images $L_1 / 2 L_0$, $L_2 / 2 L_0$ in that algebra.  The key lemma
is used at the level of lattices in $\OO^3$, not at the level of an
$\FF_2$-algebra structure inside $L_0 / 2 L_0$.

\section{What our Appendix A builds on the same identity}
% ===================================================================

Appendix~A of our paper (``The closure criterion modulo~$2$'',
\texttt{paper/main.tex}, v8) uses the same identity, in Wilson's
form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:key}
  L\bar s \cap Ls \;=\; 2 L, \tag{$\dagger$}
\end{equation}
as the foundation for an explicitly algebraic reading.
The steps:

\begin{enumerate}
\item From~\eqref{eq:key}, $2 L \subseteq L\bar s$ and $2 L \subseteq Ls$;
  applying~$\sigma$ (which fixes $2 L$, since $\sigma(L) = L$ by
  Lemma~4.1 in our paper),
  $2 L \subseteq \sigma(L\bar s)$ and $2 L \subseteq \sigma(Ls)$.
\item $L \cdot L \subseteq L$ (Coxeter; classical) and
  $\ZZ$-bilinearity descend the octonion product to an
  $\FF_2$-bilinear product $\bar\mu$ on
  $\bar L := L / 2 L \cong \FF_2^{\,8}$.
\item The sublattices $\sigma(L\bar s)$ and $\sigma(Ls)$ are full
  preimages of $4$-dimensional $\FF_2$-subspaces
  \(V := \sigma(L\bar s) / 2 L\) and \(W := \sigma(Ls) / 2 L\),
  and from $\sigma(L\bar s) + \sigma(Ls) = L$ together
  with~\eqref{eq:key} (applied after~$\sigma$),
  $\bar L = V \oplus W$.
\item The closure statements of Lemmas~4.3 and~4.4 in our paper
  become, mod~$2L$:
  \(V\) is a left ideal of $\bar L$, two-sided since
  $\sigma(L\bar s)$ is a two-sided ideal of~$L$ (Remark~4.5);
  \(W\) is a subalgebra of $\bar L$.
\item Under the natural plus-type quadratic form $q(v + 2L) :=
  \Nrm(v) / 2 \pmod 2$ on $\bar L$, direct verification shows that
  both~$V$ and~$W$ are totally isotropic, hence Lagrangians of
  $(\bar L, q)$; the decomposition $\bar L = V \oplus W$ is a
  Witt decomposition into a complementary pair of Lagrangians.
\end{enumerate}

Step~(1) uses identity~\eqref{eq:key} directly.  Step~(5) reads
$\bar L = V \oplus W$ as a polarization of~$(\bar L, q)$, an ordered
pair of complementary Lagrangians.  For Wilson's pair
$(L\bar s/2L, Ls/2L)$ that reading is prior work (Section~4), and
$\sigma$, an isometry of~$L$, maps that pair to~$(V, W)$.  What our
paper adds is the product~$\bar\mu$ of step~(2), under which $V$
and~$W$ are a two-sided ideal and a subalgebra (step~(4)).
Baez/Egan in Parts~9--10 do not take this $\FF_2$-algebra step.

\section{Attribution and overlap}
% ===================================================================

What is shared with Baez/Egan:

\begin{itemize}
\item The lattice-algebraic identity $L_1 \cap L_2 = 2 L_0$ (Egan's
  Part~9 key lemma), equivalent to Wilson's $L\bar s \cap Ls = 2 L$.
  Egan's duality-based proof of this is in~\S4 of the Verification
  and Commentary of 16~April 2026; Section~1 above summarizes it in
  one line.
\item The role of this identity in setting up a Leech-lattice
  construction inside $L_0^3$ via three sum-of-components conditions
  (Wilson 2009 in octonion language; Egan Part~9 in lattice
  language).
\end{itemize}

The polarization reading of~$\bar L$ is prior work.  Lepowsky and
Meurman (1982, \S2) identify the lattices between $2L$ and~$L$ that
are isometric to $\sqrt{2}\,L$ with the preimages of the Lagrangians
of~$(\bar L, q)$, and construct a lattice isometric to
$\sqrt{2}\,\LL$ from any polarization; their introduction notes that
work of Tits contains what amounts to the same construction.  Egan's
construction is of this form.  In Baez's series, Tim Silverman
arrived at the identification of these sublattices with the
Lagrangians independently (Part~12).  Silverman's comments of 16 and
19~February 2016 on Part~12 read Egan's construction modulo~$2$ and
find that Wilson's pair $(L\bar s/2L, Ls/2L)$ is a polarization.
David Calderbank related Egan's construction to that of Lepowsky and
Meurman (comment of 2~April 2023 on Part~9).

The main paper (\texttt{paper/main.tex}, v8) states the sublattice
identities in Wilson's setting (Section~2.3) and Egan's construction
in Section~6, for sublattices $L_1, L_2 \subset L$ isometric to
$\sqrt{2}\,L$ with $L_1 \cap L_2 = 2L$; Wilson's lattice is the case
$(L_1, L_2) = (L\bar s, Ls)$.  The paragraph ``Relation to prior
work'' of its Appendix~A gives the credits above.  This note sets out
the parallel between Egan's key lemma and Wilson's
identity~\eqref{eq:key}, on which Appendix~A builds.

\section{What it would take to close the gap further}
% ===================================================================

A natural follow-up question: does Baez/Egan's framing extend to the
$\FF_2$-algebra picture of Appendix~A?  In their setup the
generalized lattice $L_L \subset L_0^3$ is constructed without
explicit octonion multiplications; once those multiplications are
brought back in (as in Wilson 2009, and as required for our
$\bar\mu$ descent), the $\FF_2$-algebra picture is available.
Egan's Part~9 chooses to abstract away from octonion multiplication;
that abstraction is what makes our $\FF_2$-algebra reading absent
from his account.  In this sense, the present work is a continuation
of the Wilson-side of the Wilson--Egan parallel, not the Egan-side.

Conversely, the Part~10 construction (lifting $\LL$ to the
$27$-dimensional Jordan-algebra ambient $\widetilde{\LL} \subset
\mathfrak{h}_3(\OO)$, closed under $2(XY + YX)$) is a different
direction: it embeds $\LL$ into a $27$-dimensional ring,
whereas our mod-2 picture works within the $8$-dimensional $L / 2L$
inherited from~$L$ alone.  The two routes are not in competition;
they are different uses of the same shared key lemma.
