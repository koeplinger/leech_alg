# Research Statement

## English

In the Leech lattice Λ, the minimal vectors Min(Λ) — the 196,560 vectors
of squared norm 8 — play a structural role analogous to that of the root
system in root lattices.  In the E8 lattice, for example, the 240 roots
close under octonion multiplication (in the coordinate-symmetric placement
L = D₈⁺, or — for an axis-asymmetric placement such as Kirmse's J₁ — after
applying a transposition of two imaginary basis units, the Coxeter–Bruck
correction of Kirmse 1924), yielding 7 maximal orders, one for each
choice of distinguished imaginary basis element.  Whether an analogous
multiplicative structure exists on Min(Λ) — that is, whether Λ admits
an order under some bilinear product — is not known.

### References

- Conway, J.H. and Smith, D.A., "On Quaternions and Octonions" (2003),
  Chapter 9.  [Maximal orders of integral octonions.]
- Coxeter, H.S.M., "Integral Cayley numbers", Duke Math. J. 13 (1946),
  561–578.  [Correction of Kirmse's non-closed integers.]
- Wilson, R.A., "Octonions and the Leech lattice", J. Algebra 322 (2009),
  2186–2190.  [Wilson's characterisation of Λ using three copies of the
  octonion algebra.]

## German (Deutsch)

Im Leech-Gitter Λ spielen die Minimalvektoren Min(Λ) — die 196.560
Vektoren mit Normquadrat 8 — eine strukturelle Rolle, die der des
Wurzelsystems in Wurzelgittern entspricht.  Im E8-Gitter etwa schließen
die 240 Wurzeln unter Oktonionenmultiplikation (in der koordinaten­
symmetrischen Lage L = D₈⁺, oder — für eine achsenasymmetrische Lage
wie Kirmses J₁ — nach Anwendung einer Transposition zweier imaginärer
Basiseinheiten, der Coxeter–Bruck-Korrektur von Kirmse 1924), und es
ergeben sich 7 Maximalordnungen, je eine pro Wahl eines ausgezeichneten
imaginären Basiselements.  Ob eine analoge multiplikative Struktur
auf Min(Λ) existiert — das heißt, ob Λ unter einem bilinearen Produkt
eine Ordnung zulässt — ist nicht bekannt.

## Corrections applied to the user's original draft

1. **"Min(Λ) takes the role of a root system"** → Corrected: Min(Λ) plays
   a structural role *analogous to* a root system.  Technically, a root
   system requires norm-2 vectors and closure under reflections; the Leech
   lattice has no norm-2 vectors.  Min(Λ) consists of norm-4 vectors
   (squared norm 8) that generate Λ and determine its symmetry group, hence
   the analogy.  (Here "squared norm 8" refers to the standard Euclidean
   inner product v · v = Σ vᵢ²; Wilson uses a halved norm under which these
   vectors have norm 4.  This project uses the standard convention
   throughout; see terminology.md, "Leech lattice Λ — norm conventions.")

2. **"Kirmse-twist"** → Clarified as **a transposition of two imaginary
   basis units**, a linear involution of R⁸.  Kirmse (1924) exhibited
   a candidate lattice J₁ for an integral-octonion order that is not
   closed under multiplication.  Coxeter (1946, §4) records the remedy
   due to R. H. Bruck: "Bruck's domain J can be derived from J₁ by
   transposing two of the i's."  The 21 such transpositions yield the
   seven maximal orders.  The term "Kirmse twist" is used in this
   project as shorthand for this transposition.  (An earlier draft of
   this statement used "index-doubling permutation"; that was a
   misconception, corrected from the primary sources — see
   terminology.md, "Kirmse integers — Kirmse twist".)

3. **"integral orders"** → Corrected to "maximal orders."  A maximal order
   is a lattice in the octonion algebra that is closed under multiplication,
   contains the identity, and is maximal with respect to inclusion.  The 7
   orders obtained via the Kirmse construction are maximal.  "Integral order"
   is less precise.
