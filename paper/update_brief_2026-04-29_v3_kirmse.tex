\begin{abstract}
The manuscript version of 29 April 2026 (v3) is frozen while under review.
This is the \emph{second}, separate update brief for that frozen version; it
is not to be merged with the first
(\texttt{update\_brief\_2026-04-29\_v3.tex}, a footnote correction and an
appendix simplification).  The present brief concerns one topic: how the
manuscript describes the Kirmse--Coxeter correction of the integral
octonions (the ``Kirmse twist'').  The four primary sources have now been
obtained and verified from scratch in exact arithmetic; the brief is no
longer provisional.  It records what was verified and the resulting
corrections, and supplies replacement text.

\section{Verified from primary sources}\label{sec:verified}
% ===============================================================

Each source was read in the original and re-derived computationally;
no secondary source was taken on trust.  Verification notes are in
\texttt{paper/reviews/}; scripts in \texttt{python\_project/src/}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Kirmse (1924).}  He gives a correct octonion multiplication
  table and seeks the maximal orders.  He states there are \emph{eight}
  (``Man findet acht solche \ldots,'' p.~70) and exhibits one, $J_1$, in
  detail.  \emph{$J_1$ is not closed under multiplication}: the product
  $\tfrac12(1{+}i_1{+}i_2{+}i_3)\cdot\tfrac12(1{+}i_1{+}i_4{+}i_5)
  =\tfrac12(i_1{+}i_2{+}i_4{+}i_7)$ leaves $J_1$.  The true number of
  maximal orders is \emph{seven}; $J_1$ is the spurious entry.
  (\texttt{verify\_kirmse\_1924.py}; note 2026-05-20.)
\item \textbf{Dickson (1923).}  Predating Coxeter, Dickson gave a genuine
  integral-octonion construction -- his order $O_D$ \emph{is} closed.  His
  Theorem~XV nonetheless undercounts (states three maximal orders; there are
  seven), because of an extra assumption he states openly in the derivation:
  that the orders contain the Hurwitz quaternions.  (\texttt{verify\_dickson%
  \_1923.py}; note 2026-05-20.)
\item \textbf{Mahler (1942).}  Ideal theory over Coxeter's order; results
  correct, one misprint.  (\texttt{verify\_mahler\_1942.py}; note
  2026-05-20.)
\item \textbf{Coxeter (1946).}  Section~4 identifies Kirmse's error
  (``Actually there are only seven''), with the \emph{same} non-closure
  witness as above.  The remedy is due to \textbf{R.\,H.~Bruck}: ``Bruck's
  domain $J$ can be derived from $J_1$ by transposing two of the $i$'s.''
  That is, a transposition of two \emph{imaginary} octonion units -- a
  linear involution of~$\RR^8$.  The $\binom72=21$ such transpositions yield
  the seven maximal orders.  Coxeter's postscript independently identifies
  the cause of Dickson's undercount (the integral-quaternion assumption).
  (\texttt{verify\_coxeter\_1946.py}; note 2026-05-22.)
\end{itemize}

\section{Corrections to the manuscript}\label{sec:items}
% ===============================================================

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textbf{The correcting operation.}  It is a transposition of two
  \emph{imaginary coordinate axes} -- a linear involution of~$\RR^8$, the
  same \emph{kind} of map as the manuscript's~$\sigma$.  Any wording that
  describes it otherwise (e.g.\ as interchanging the identity with an
  imaginary unit, or as a Fano-triple permutation that is ``not induced by a
  linear map'') is to be removed.

\item[(b)] \textbf{Credit Bruck.}  Coxeter \emph{found} the error; Bruck
  \emph{supplied} the remedy; Coxeter published and developed it.  The
  manuscript credits only Coxeter (Remark in Section~2.3).  Add Bruck.

\item[(c)] \textbf{Remark ``$\sigma$ vs.\ a Fano-line permutation''
  (Section~3).}  It asserts that Coxeter's correction ``rewrites the
  multiplication table without being induced by a linear map on~$\RR^8$.''
  This is \textbf{false}: Coxeter's correction \emph{is} a linear coordinate
  transposition.  Replace the Remark with the text of
  Section~\ref{sec:replacement}.

\item[(d)] \textbf{Section~6 historical paragraph.}  It carries the same
  false claim (``Coxeter's correction is a permutation of Fano \emph{triples}
  \ldots\ without being induced by a linear map on~$\RR^8$'').  The
  ``different strata'' framing there is correct and is kept; the
  ``different \emph{kind} of map'' / ``not a linear map'' contrast is to be
  replaced by the accurate statement: the two are the same kind of map (a
  coordinate transposition), differing in the lattice each acts on --
  Coxeter's on a maximal-order candidate, the present~$\sigma$ on the
  sublattice~$Ls$.

\item[(e)] \textbf{Remark on Kirmse's convention (Section~2.3).}  It says
  ``Kirmse originally proposed a specific labelling under which $D_8^+$ is
  not closed.''  This locates the error in the multiplication table;
  Coxeter's Section~4 -- and our verification -- locate it in the
  \emph{lattice} Kirmse exhibited ($J_1$): his table is itself a correct
  octonion algebra.  Re-word so the error is the choice of lattice, and so
  that Kirmse is recorded as stating \emph{eight} maximal domains where
  there are seven.

\item[(f)] \textbf{Bibliography.}  Add Dickson (1923); optionally Mahler
  (1942).  A broader historical appendix (treating all four sources) is a
  separate deliverable; this brief covers only the wording of the twist.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Replacement text}\label{sec:replacement}
% ===============================================================

The following replaces the Remark ``$\sigma$ vs.\ a Fano-line permutation.''

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Remark} ($\sigma$ and the Coxeter correction).
The Coxeter--Bruck correction of Kirmse's integral-octonion convention and
the present~$\sigma$ are the same kind of map: a transposition of two
imaginary coordinate axes, a linear involution of~$\RR^8$.  Like any
coordinate transposition, each rewrites the multiplication table -- it does
not preserve the Fano line structure -- but each \emph{is} induced by a
linear map.  The two differ only in what they act on.  Coxeter's
transposition repairs a maximal-order candidate that sits asymmetrically
in~$\RR^8$.  The present~$\sigma$ acts where the order is $L = D_8^+$, which
is invariant under every coordinate permutation; hence $\sigma(L) = L$ and
\[
  L \cdot_\sigma L \;=\; \sigma(L \cdot L) \;\subseteq\; L,
\]
so the twist preserves the $E_8$-level closure automatically.  The twist's
effect falls entirely on the sublattices $Ls$ and $L\bar s$, which are not
$\sigma$-invariant: that is what redirects Wilson's sublattice conditions.
\end{quote}

\noindent
The Section~6 paragraph is corrected to the same effect, keeping its
``different strata'' language: Coxeter's transposition and~$\sigma$ are one
kind of operation, applied at different strata -- Coxeter's to the $E_8$
order, $\sigma$ to the condition-(3) sublattice.
