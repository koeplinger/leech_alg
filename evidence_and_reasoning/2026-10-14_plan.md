## 1. What the index findings mean

Feedback on the author's reading at prompt 288.

1. **Index 1 means the products generate $\Lambda$, not that every point is a
   product.**  Every lattice vector is an integer combination of products.
   Most minimal vectors are provably not single products: the block sum of
   $u \star_g v$ is a product of two vectors of $Ls$, so its norm lies in
   $16\mathbb Z$, while 189,360 of the 196,560 minimal vectors have block
   sums of norm 4, 8 or 12 (note, Section 3.1).
2. **What moves is the twist, not a basis.**  A twist $g$ changes the
   product; equivalently, Wilson's two sublattices move inside a fixed $L$.
   The index does not depend on any basis of $\Lambda$.
3. **Where index 1 comes from: a misaligned unit.**  The twisted product has
   the unit $1_g = g^{-1}(e_0)$, which is not in $L$.  The index exceeds 1
   when $2 \cdot 1_g$ lies in one of Wilson's sublattices: then a
   homomorphism $\Lambda \to \mathbb Z/2$ (the parity of a component along
   $1_g$) vanishes on every product but not on $\Lambda$.  It equals 1 when
   $2 \cdot 1_g$ lies in neither.  The first direction has this short proof;
   the second, and the exact values, are computed exhaustively (the review;
   re-derived at prompt 287; random samples at prompt 288), not proved.
   $\sigma$ and every coordinate permutation fix $e_0$, and
   $2e_0 = s\bar s \in L\bar s$; the index-1 reflections move $2\cdot 1_g$ out
   of both sublattices.  So index 1 goes with the unit being out of
   alignment with the sublattices, not with lattice points aligned to it.
4. **The idempotents, precisely.**  $6(\varepsilon_i - \tfrac13\omega) \in
   \Lambda$ exactly when $2\cdot 1_g \in L\bar s$, and $2\omega \in \Lambda$
   exactly when $2\cdot 1_g \in Ls$ (a direct check of Wilson's conditions).
   So the index exceeds 1 when, and (by the computation) only when, one of
   these idempotents has its sixfold multiple in $\Lambda$; for $\sigma$,
   $6(\varepsilon_i - \tfrac13\omega) \in \Lambda$ (paper, Section 5.2).
   Unit-like elements exist, but not in $\Lambda$: $D$ is a copy of the
   octonions with unit $\tfrac13\Delta(1_g)$, and $T$ has none (these are the
   "hybrid subspaces"); $\varepsilon_1$ acts as a block permutation, so on
   the overlattice $\Lambda + \mathbb Z\varepsilon_1$ every point is a single
   product, $v = \varepsilon_1 \star \operatorname{swap}(v)$.  Inside
   $\Lambda$ no element multiplies $\Lambda$ onto itself (determinant 0 or
   divisible by 16; review, Section 3.2).
5. **Maximality concerns what lies above $\Lambda$, not products escaping
   it.**  Closure (products of vectors of $\Lambda$ stay in $\Lambda$) is the
   theorem.  The order is not maximal because larger lattices are closed too:
   $L^3$ for every twist by an isometry of $L$, and $\Lambda +
   \mathbb Z\varepsilon_1$ (claims 016, 018).  No twist changes this, and
   nothing needs fixing: a maximal order above $\Lambda$ exists, but it is
   decomposable and not integral (claim 020).
6. **The name "ozones" / "ozone algebra"** names a well-defined object, since
   the ambient algebras of all twists are isomorphic.  "Ozone group" is an
   established term in noncommutative algebra since 2023 (prompt 288 records
   the source).  Recommendation: keep the name out of v8; record it in
   `terminology.md` once adopted (Manifesto §9).

## 2. Why the span index earns one sentence

- For a unital order the index is always 1; here it carries information.
- It is invariant under ring isomorphism.  With the values $2^{16}$, $2^{10}$
  and 1, the twists $\sigma$, any even closing permutation and the
  reflection make $\Lambda$ into three pairwise nonisomorphic orders,
  although their ambient algebras are all isomorphic: the choice of twist is
  not cosmetic.  This is the sentence for the paper.
- It answers the paper's own Section 7 question, which cannot stay posed.
- It bears neither on closure nor on maximality.

Recommendation: keep it, compact, inside the census subsection, as the
author proposes.  Dropping it altogether would also mean editing the abstract
and Section 6: more change, not less.
