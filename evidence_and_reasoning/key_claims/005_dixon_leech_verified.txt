Title: Dixon's octonionic construction gives the 196,560 minimal vectors of a Leech lattice: the componentwise conjugates of Wilson's minimal vectors
Status: ESTABLISHED

Date: 2026-04-07

--- STATEMENT ---

Dixon [Dixon2010 Section 6] characterizes the Leech lattice minimal-vector
shell in three families:

  ℓ₀ = ½(e₀+e₁+...+e₇),  N(ℓ₀) = 2  [Section 3, eq. (6)]
  A^odd  = A₁ ∪ A₃  (240 norm-2 elements)
  A^even = Ξ^even   (240 norm-1 elements)

  Type 1 [eq. (22)]:  (2P, 0, 0) + 2 cyclic perms,  P ∈ A^odd
  Type 2 [eq. (21)]:  (2Q, ±2e_aQ, 0) + 2 cyclic perms,  Q ∈ A^even,
                       a ∈ {0,...,7}
  Type 3 [eq. (20)]:  (P, ±e_aP, ±e_a(ℓ₀(e_cP))) + 2 cyclic perms,
                       P ∈ A^odd, a,c ∈ {0,...,7}, ± signs independent

  Count: 720 + 11,520 + 184,320 = 196,560.

All products are the octonion product of Dixon's table (3),
e_a e_{a+1} = e_{a+3}.  Dixon prints the type-3 third component also as
(e_aℓ₀e_a)(e_bP), with e_b depending on e_a, e_c and P; the two forms give
the same set of vectors.  The nesting matters: (e_aℓ₀)(e_cP) is in general a
different octonion, and with it the type-3 vectors have inner products 1, 3,
5 and 6.
Taking "all permutations" of the components, as Dixon writes, gives the
same set as the three cyclic shifts.

Computationally verified, exactly:
  (i)   All 196,560 vectors are distinct and have N_std = 8.
  (ii)  Their componentwise conjugates (x̄, ȳ, z̄) are exactly Wilson's
        196,560 minimal vectors (claim 004), family by family.  Dixon
        derives eq. (20) from Wilson's largest family in this way: conjugate
        each component, shift cyclically, replace P by e_cP [Dixon2010
        Section 6].
  (iii) Independently of Wilson's construction: the Z-span of the vectors,
        rescaled to norm 4, is even and unimodular of rank 24, with no
        vector of norm 2 and exactly 196,560 vectors of norm 4.  It is
        therefore the Leech lattice (Conway's uniqueness theorem), and the
        196,560 vectors are all of its minimal vectors.

--- A^ODD vs WILSON'S E8 ROOTS ---

Dixon's A^odd = A₁ ∪ A₃, where:
  A₁ (112 elements):  ±e_a ± e_b, identical to Wilson's type-1 roots.
  A₃ (128 elements):  ½∑_{a=0}^{7} ±e_a with an EVEN number of minus signs.

Wilson's E8 roots = A₁ ∪ {½∑±e_a with an ODD number of minus signs}.

The two 128-element half-integer families (A₃ vs Wilson-type-2) are disjoint
(they partition all 2^8 = 256 sign patterns).  Conjugation changes the sign
of seven coordinates and so maps A^odd onto Wilson's E8 roots.

--- RELATION TO WILSON'S CONSTRUCTION ---

Dixon's shell and Wilson's shell are different subsets of R^24 (Wilson's
lies in L³, Dixon's in L̄³, with L̄ the conjugate of L), related by
componentwise conjugation (x, y, z) ↦ (x̄, ȳ, z̄), an isometry
of R^24.  Dixon's lattice is the conjugate of Wilson's Λ.  Most of Dixon's
vectors lie outside Wilson's Λ; their conjugates lie in it.  For type 1:
  - P ∈ A₁: all 336 satisfy Wilson's is_in_leech. ✓
  - P ∈ A₃: all 384 FAIL Wilson's is_in_leech.
    Reason: P ∈ A₃ has an even number of minus signs, hence an even
    coordinate sum, so P ∉ L and 2P ∉ 2L = Ls̄ ∩ Ls.  2P lies in Ls̄ (every
    vector with all coordinates ±1 does), so Wilson's condition 2 holds and
    condition 3, 2P ∈ Ls, fails.

--- NORM VERIFICATION ---

Type-1: N_std(2P, 0, 0) = 4·N(P) = 4·2 = 8. ✓
Type-2: N_std(2Q, ±2e_aQ, 0) = N(2Q) + N(2e_aQ) = 4·1 + 4·1·1 = 8. ✓
Type-3: N_std(P, ±e_aP, ±e_a(ℓ₀(e_cP)))
       = N(P) + N(e_a)·N(P) + N(e_a)·N(ℓ₀)·N(e_c)·N(P)
       = 2 + 2 + 4 = 8. ✓

--- ZERO-COMPONENT STRUCTURE ---

Type-1: exactly 1 nonzero R^8 block (2 zero blocks).
Type-2: exactly 2 nonzero R^8 blocks (1 zero block).
Type-3: exactly 3 nonzero R^8 blocks (0 zero blocks).

This structure is analogous to Wilson's construction and ensures the three
families are pairwise disjoint by inspection.

--- NOTE ON ALGEBRAIC STRUCTURE ---

The user's question asks whether Dixon's construction can be interpreted as a
24-dimensional integral algebra that is a module over the integers closing on
the minimal-vector shell.

Dixon [Dixon2010 Section 7] explicitly addresses this question:
  "The question is: can Λ₂₄ be given its own algebraic structure, perhaps
  involving a ternary multiplication?  No idea yet."

The computational tools established here (leech_dixon.py) provide the
reference implementation for testing any proposed product.  Wilson's
membership test (is_in_leech in leech_wilson.py) provides the reference
lattice against which a proposed product can be evaluated.

--- ANSWER TO PROMPT 008 ---

1. Dixon's construction produces 196,560 distinct norm-8 vectors, and they
   are the minimal vectors of a Leech lattice (statement (iii)).

2. Dixon's shell is the componentwise conjugate of Wilson's shell; as
   subsets of R^24 the two shells differ.

3. Dixon Section 7 poses the question of a 24-dimensional integral algebra
   structure on Λ₂₄ as open; claim 008 answers it for Wilson's Λ (paper
   Theorem 1.1) with an order whose product does not preserve the minimal
   shell (paper Remark 1.3).
