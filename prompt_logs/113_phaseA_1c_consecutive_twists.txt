(1c) task: examine whether consecutive sigma-twists close on the Leech
lattice. Two sigma-twists compose to a single twist by pi = sigma2*sigma1
(conjugation twist x.pi y = pi^-1(pi(x).pi(y))). A product of two
transpositions is identity, a 3-cycle (share one index), or a (2,2)-element
(disjoint). The triple product star_pi closes on Lambda iff (paper's proof
structure) pi(Ls).pi(Ls) <= pi(Ls) AND L.pi(Lsbar) <= pi(Lsbar). Census of
all permutations of {1..7} by cycle type. Status: computing.

OUTCOME (verify_consecutive_twists_exact.py, verify_twist_characterization.py):
  - First attempt: a lemma criterion (pi(Ls).pi(Ls)<=pi(Ls) AND
    L.pi(Lsbar)<=pi(Lsbar)). The 2nd lemma is sufficient but NOT necessary
    (the three terms of a pairwise block sum are coupled by Lambda-membership).
    A direct cross-check on Lambda caught a 3-cycle closing although the
    criterion said no. Criterion discarded.
  - Reliable test: built a genuine Z-basis of Lambda (24 vectors; covolume
    2^12 verified). star_pi closes <=> all 576 basis-pair products in Lambda.
  - RESULT (exact): identity twist -> no; single transposition -> all 21
    close; 3-cycles -> 35 of 70 close; (2,2) doubles -> 42 of 105 close.
    Consecutive sigma-twists DO close on Lambda, for a substantial fraction.
  - CHARACTERISATION (Fano plane governs it): for every 3-subset exactly one
    of the two 3-cycle orientations closes; a (2,2) (a b)(c d) never closes
    when its three fixed indices form a Fano line (0/21) and closes for
    exactly half otherwise (42/84).
