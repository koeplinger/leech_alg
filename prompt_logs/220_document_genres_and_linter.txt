MY PROPOSED RULE 4 IS WITHDRAWN. It was wrong. The denormalized, indexing and
summarizing artifacts are deliberate: they serve a human reader AND they are
the cheap navigation layer that keeps an AI from having to read the whole
corpus. A consistency sweep must therefore be free to ADD to them when they
have fallen behind. What went wrong in prompt 218 was not that content was
added; it was that content of the WRONG GENRE was added to the WRONG
ARTIFACTS. The genre spec is the fix, not a ban on adding.

1. DOCUMENT_GENRES.md (top level). Three genres, per the user's taxonomy:

   IMMUTABLE   prompt_logs/**. Never edited. A correction goes in a LATER entry.

   FROZEN      A dated record of what was true at a point in time:
                 released paper versions (paper/main_YYYY-MM-DD.tex) and PDFs
                 companion, supplemental and standalone notes
                 key_claims/NNN_*.txt
                 paper/reviews/**
                 the version changelogs
                 trial_NNN_results.md (when the trial is closed)
                 research plans (once engaged)
                 computer programs (once referenced by a paper, note or claim)
               Changed ONLY by a dated addendum, or marked deprecated and
               superseded. Never rewritten in place. Rationale: a frozen
               artifact is EVIDENCE; it records what was believed on the day it
               was written, and a reader must see both the belief and its
               correction.

   CURRENT     Describes what IS. Edited freely, in place, with NO notice, NO
   STATE       date, NO supersession narrative. Git is its history.
                 MANIFESTO.md, DOCUMENT_GENRES.md, TRIAL_METHODOLOGY.md,
                 CURRENT_STATE.md, every indexing README.md, terminology.md,
                 editorial_standards.md, the licenses
               Special case: research_statement.md is updated only on a
               SIGNIFICANT deviation of the research goal.

   Plus a section recording the user's correction of my proposed rule 4:
   DENORMALIZATION IS DELIBERATE. The indexes, summaries and ledgers serve a
   human reader AND are the cheap navigation layer for an AI. A sweep MAY and
   SHOULD add to them. The failure mode is not "documentation grew"; it is
   "documentation started narrating its own history."

2. tools/lint_docs.py + tools/README.md. Mechanical enforcement; no LLM, no
   network, no dependencies; runs in about a second; exit 1 on any error.
   Five checks:
     1 GENRE     banned phrases in current-state documents (dated correction
                 notices, settlement dates, supersession narratives,
                 change-history footers). The three SPEC files
                 (DOCUMENT_GENRES.md, MANIFESTO.md, tools/README.md) are exempt
                 from THIS check only: a rule book must be able to name the
                 thing it forbids.
     2 IMMUTABLE a git-tracked prompt log that is modified or deleted -> error.
     3 FROZEN    a frozen artifact rewritten in place without a dated addendum
                 -> warning.
     4 DANGLING  every repo path named in a current-state document must exist.
     5 INDEX     every program in python_project/src/ and gap_project/ must
                 appear in the README that indexes it.
   TESTED against a deliberately broken CURRENT_STATE.md: it caught the dated
   correction notice, the supersession narrative, and BOTH dangling paths
   (including main_2026-07-10.tex, the exact reference that went stale this
   week), and exited 1. Repo currently clean: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

4. MANIFESTO Section 14 (Subagents), per the user's instruction: a subagent gets
   NO WRITE ACCESS TO PROSE. It may read, search, compute, run programs and
   report. Every edit is made by the main thread and shown to the human. A

   the cost of reviewing them lands on the human. A sweep reports before it
   writes.
