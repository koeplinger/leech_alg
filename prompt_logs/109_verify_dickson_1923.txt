The paper is in ENGLISH (French journal). The octonion section is
Section 19, "The Integers of Cayley's Algebra" (journal pp. ~319-326).

First-pass survey (pdftotext):
  - Section 19: Dickson uses quaternion units 1,i,j,k plus e,ie,je,ke;
    Cayley-Dickson "condensed" product (his eq. 24); norm; N(xy)=N(x)N(y).
  - He determines all maximal orders ("maximal sets of integers ... having
    properties C,U,N"), derives the half-integer structure, builds an
    explicit 8-element basis (29) and the order (30).
  - He ASSERTS closure ("It can be verified that this is true also of the
    product of any two of the numbers (29)") and PROVES maximality (no
    enlargement keeps C,U,N).
  - THEOREM XV: "The only maximal systems of integers of Cayley's algebra
    having properties C,U,N are (30) and the two systems obtained from it by
    cyclic permutations of i,j,k" -- i.e. Dickson claims exactly THREE.
  - Closing remark hedges: "other maximal systems (if such exist) having
    properties C,U1,N,M".
  Note: modern count is seven; Kirmse claimed eight; Dickson claims three.
  To be verified from scratch: (a) is Dickson's order (30) genuinely closed;
  (b) how many maximal orders exist for his multiplication; (c) whether "3"
  is an undercount or a consequence of his C,U,N axioms.

OUTCOME (verified, python_project/src/verify_dickson_1923.py):
  - Dickson's Cayley-Dickson product (eq.24) is a genuine octonion algebra.
  - Dickson's order O_D (system (30), basis (29)) IS closed -- a genuine
    maximal order. Dickson got the CONSTRUCTION right (unlike Kirmse's J_1).
    Confirms Petersson: isomorphic to Coxeter's.
  - There are exactly 7 maximal orders for Dickson's product; all 7 satisfy
    his axioms C (closure), U (contains 1), N (integral norm).
  - THEOREM XV claims 3 -> UNDERCOUNT (3 vs 7).
  - Cause, stated by Dickson himself (p.321): "We shall assume that the
    latter [Hurwitz quaternions] occur in our set in all cases." Exactly 3
    of the 7 maximal orders contain p=1/2(1+i+j+k); those 3 are O_D and its
    two (ijk)-cyclic images = Theorem XV's triple. The 4 missed all lack p.
    Theorem XV as printed is false; the proof establishes the true RESTRICTED
    statement (only 3 maximal orders contain the Hurwitz quaternions).
  - Side by side: Dickson 1923 -- construction correct, count 3 (under by 4);
    Kirmse 1924 -- construction wrong (J_1 not closed), count 8 (over by 1);
    truth 7.
