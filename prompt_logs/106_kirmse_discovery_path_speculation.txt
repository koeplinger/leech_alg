Prompt 106 — Assessing a proposed "path to discovery" narrative for Kirmse
Date: 2026-05-20

User request (verbatim, abridged):
"Kirmse may have written down one concrete realization that was indeed an
integral order. It worked. He then looked at how this could possibly be
generalized. He arrived at the wrong generalization formula (that actually
yields 30 possible realizations). In a second incorrect conclusion, he then
counted eight integral orders ... Do you think this could have been a
plausible path to discovery, wrongful generalization, and subsequent
erroneous 'generalized' exposition?"

Assessment given (no files changed; discussion turn):
  - Plausible only as a GENERIC research pattern; as a claim about Kirmse it
    is speculation the paper does not support.
  - Its load-bearing step ("a first concrete realization that worked") has
    no evidence: the paper records exactly one concrete realization, J_1,
    and J_1 does NOT work. The text presents the general result first, then
    specializes to J_1 -- that is exposition order, not discovery order;
    the discovery order is not in the document.
  - Mathematical mischaracterisations in the narrative:
     * the schematic/"generalization formula" is NOT wrong -- it is a correct
       NECESSARY form (all 7 genuine orders have it); "30" is our count of
       lattices meeting that correct form, not the yield of a wrong formula,
       and not a number Kirmse wrote.
     * "counted eight, assuming the original was among them" does not explain
       WHY eight; the eight is explained by the unit-invariance reconstruction.
  - The narrative is internally tense: Step 1 says the original "worked";
    Step 4 places the original among the eight, which contain the
    non-working J_1.
  - Key point: the attribution conclusion does NOT depend on the path. What
    the paper establishes stands regardless of how Kirmse arrived at it.
  - Recommendation: keep the write-up to the verified logical anatomy
    (two-part closure test, part two omitted); state explicitly that the
    paper does not record Kirmse's path of discovery; do not include a
    discovery-path narrative (or flag it heavily as speculation).
