### 1.2 The automorphism group, settled (§5.3)

- **What changed.** v5 recorded only the empirical bound $S_3 \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda,+,\star) \subsetneq \mathrm{Co}_0$ and posed full identification as open. It is now determined: $\mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda,+,\star)$ is **finite of order 36, with structure $C_6 \times S_3$**. The $S_3$ is the block permutation of Definition 3.3; the $C_6$ is generated by the signed permutation of imaginary units $e_1 \mapsto -e_5$, $e_2 \mapsto -e_7$, $e_3 \mapsto e_2$, $e_4 \mapsto -e_4$, $e_5 \mapsto e_6$, $e_6 \mapsto e_1$, $e_7 \mapsto -e_3$ fixing $e_0$. Every automorphism is a blockwise octonion automorphism followed by a block permutation.

  The subsection also settles the ambient question first: $\mathbb{R}^{24} = \mathbb{O}^3$ splits as $D \oplus T$ with $D = \{(a,a,a) : a \in \mathbb{O}\}$ (dimension 8) and $T = \{(p,q,r) : p+q+r = 0\}$ (dimension 16) mutually annihilating two-sided ideals, whence $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^{24}, \star) = G_2 \times G_2 \times S_3$. The containment $\mathrm{Aut}(\Lambda,+,\star) \subsetneq \mathrm{Co}_0$ is thereby **proved, not asserted**: a real octonion automorphism preserves the positive-definite norm and $D \perp T$, so every $\star$-automorphism of $\mathbb{R}^{24}$ is orthogonal; strictness follows because $-I_{24} \in \mathrm{Co}_0$ does not preserve $\star$. Completeness of the order-36 claim is established by two independent routes in the standalone note (§5 below): Route 1 via the classification of the automorphisms of the complexification of $(T,\star)$ (non-enumerative), Route 2 entirely finite and machine-checkable. A 12 July draft sentence stating a completeness caveat in the paper itself was removed in the 15 July pass; the final paper states the result without qualification and cites the note.
- **Where.** New §5.3; the §7 automorphism bullet (§2 below).
- **Rationale.** Review finding R3 objected that v5 asserted the $\mathrm{Co}_0$ containment without proof. Rather than retreat to a weaker phrasing, the containment was proved, and the group computed. Scripts: `verify_aut_lambda_star.py`, `verify_aut_octonion_crosscheck.py`, `verify_ideal_decomposition.py`, `verify_star_algebra_structure.py` (in `python_project/src/`), and `aut_lambda_star.g`, `octonion_stabilisers.g` with their generator files (in `gap_project/`). The derivation is written out in full in the standalone note (§5 below).

### 1.3 Idempotents, classified (§5.2)

- **What changed.** New subsection. The ambient algebra $(\mathbb{R}^{24}, +, \star)$ has **exactly eight** idempotents: $0$; $\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \varepsilon_3$ (the block units $(e_0,0,0)$ etc.); $\tfrac13\omega$ with $\omega = \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 + \varepsilon_3$; and $\varepsilon_i - \tfrac13\omega$. Every block of each is a multiple of $e_0$: no imaginary part, no positive-dimensional family. None of the seven nonzero idempotents lies in Λ (because $e_0 \notin L$), so **the order has no nonzero idempotent**. The passage from algebra to lattice is a rescaling: the least positive lattice multiples are $4\varepsilon_i$ ($N = 16$), $6(\varepsilon_i - \tfrac13\omega)$ ($N = 24$) and $4\omega$ ($N = 48$), each satisfying $u \star u = n\,u$ for its own $n$. (The $-8\varepsilon_1$ minimal-shell witness of the 12 July remark is not carried in the final subsection; it remains in the claim ledger and the script.)
- **Where.** New §5.2.
- **Rationale.** v5 could say only that no *minimal* vector is idempotent. The classification settles the question on all of Λ and on the ambient algebra. Scripts: `verify_idempotent_classification.py`, `verify_idempotent_lattice_rescaling.py`.

### 1.4 Square-zero elements: a v5 claim rescoped (§5.4)

- **What changed.** New subsection, and it corrects the reading of a v5 statement. In the ambient algebra, $u = (x,y,z)$ satisfies $u \star u = 0$ exactly when $x, y, z$ are purely imaginary, of equal norm, and sum to zero (a hexagonal triple in $\mathrm{Im}(\mathbb{O}) = \mathbb{R}^7$): a 12-dimensional cone inside $T$, meeting $D$ only in 0. **Λ does contain nonzero square-zero elements**: 4,032 of them of norm 12, from the hexagonal triples of the $E_7$ root system formed by the 126 norm-4 vectors of $L\bar{s} \cap \mathrm{Im}(\mathbb{O})$. The exhaustive minimal-shell search reported in v5 could not have found them, for an arithmetic reason now stated: $N(u) = 3N(x)$ with $N(x) \in 2\mathbb{Z}$ and every Λ-norm in $4\mathbb{Z}$, so every square-zero vector of Λ has norm in $12\mathbb{Z}$, while the minimal norm is 8. Following the 17 July review (blocker B1 of §8.2), the statement is scoped stratum by stratum: the 4,032 root triples are **exactly the norm-12 stratum**, and higher strata are realized, e.g. the blockwise sum of two blockwise-orthogonal root triples is square-zero of norm 24 (`verify_square_zero_norm24_witness.py`).
- **Where.** New §5.4. The deleted v5 remark's surviving content ("neither 0 nor 8 occurs among the product norms, so no minimal vector is idempotent and none squares to zero") is now one sentence of Remark 5.1, which points forward to §5.2 and §5.4 for the unrestricted answers.
- **Rationale.** The v5 sentence "the minimal shell contains no idempotents and no square nilpotents" was true as written but invited the unscoped reading "Λ has no nilpotents", which is **false**. The scoped statement is the only one the paper now makes. Script: `verify_square_zero_classification.py`.

### 1.5 Closure of permutation cycles: the full $S_7$ census (§5.6)

- **What changed.** New subsection with an exhaustive table over **all of $S_7$**. Writing $x \cdot_\pi y := \pi^{-1}(\pi(x) \cdot \pi(y))$ (for the involution σ this is the product of Definition 3.2, since $\sigma^{-1} = \sigma$) and testing closure of the assembled triple product exactly on the 576 basis-pair products of a ℤ-basis of Λ, all 5,040 permutations are enumerated, grouped by the fourteen non-identity cycle types: transpositions 21/21 (100%), 3-cycles 35/70 (50%), $(2,2)$ 42/105 (40%), 4-cycles 0/210 (0%), $(3,2)$ 126/420 (30%), 5-cycles 252/504 (50%), $(2,2,2)$ 21/105 (20%), $(3,3)$ 112/280 (40%), $(4,2)$ 294/630 (46.7%), 6-cycles 210/840 (25%), $(3,2,2)$ 105/210 (50%), $(4,3)$ 168/420 (40%), $(5,2)$ 42/504 (8.3%), 7-cycles 336/720 (46.7%); total 1,764/5,039 (35.0%). The identity (the untwisted product) does not close. The counts are exhaustive, not sampled. The 4-cycles fail without exception; for the 3-cycles the closing half admits a description (one of the two cyclic orientations per three-element index subset). Terminology: a general π is a *permutation*; *twist* is reserved for transpositions.
- **Where.** New §5.6 (*Closure of permutation cycles*). Remark 4.9 of v5 (consecutive twists, with the $(2,2)$ count) is deleted; the census table subsumes it.

The displayed formula initially carried π outside; the 18 July forensic review found that the scripts compute $\pi^{-1}$ outside (the two coincide exactly on involutions, so Definition 3.2, §4, and Theorem 1.1 were never affected) and the display was corrected, with no change to any count (§8.3). Scripts: `verify_all_permutations_exact.py` (all 5,040, reusing the exact per-permutation test of `verify_consecutive_twists_exact.py`); earlier partial runs `verify_all_cycles_exact.py`, `consistency_checks.py`.
