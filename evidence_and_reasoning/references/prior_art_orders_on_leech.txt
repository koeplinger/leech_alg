Topic: Prior art — multiplicative structures / orders on the Leech lattice
=============================================================================

Created: 2026-04-11.  Updated: 2026-10-31.

Literature search conducted: 2026-04-11, extended 2026-04-14
Purpose: Identify any prior work establishing or ruling out an order (or
ring, algebra, multiplicatively closed structure) on the Leech lattice,
before publishing our finding.

---

## Summary of search

**No prior work was found establishing an order on the Leech lattice
under an octonion-derived bilinear product.**

The specific construction in this project appears in no prior work: a
Z₃-symmetric triple-octonion product on R²⁴, with σ a coordinate
transposition of two imaginary basis units that is an octonion-algebra
isomorphism fixing the E₈ lattice L = D₈⁺ but moving Wilson's
sublattices Ls and Ls̄.

The closest related work falls into four categories (listed
roughly by structural distance from the present construction):

1. **Baez / Egan (2014)**: Explores whether the Leech lattice can be
   made into a ring via the exceptional Jordan algebra (27-dimensional,
   commutative).  Finds that an integer sublattice of 𝔥₃(O) containing
   a Leech embedding is closed under the doubled Jordan product
   2(xy + yx), but this involves a factor-of-2 scaling and the Jordan
   (commutative) product, not an octonion-derived (noncommutative,
   nonassociative) product.  The question of closure under the
   undoubled Jordan product is left open, with a conjectured negative
   answer.  Egan also constructs Λ from E₈ alone (part 9): Λ(L₁, L₂) for
   two sublattices of E₈ isometric to √2 E₈ that meet in 2E₈.  Wilson's Λ
   and σ(Λ) are two of its 17,280 lattices, and by the paper's closure
   criterion (Proposition 5.3) the untwisted product closes on Λ(L₁, L₂)
   exactly when L₁ and L₂ are closed under the standard octonion product
   (paper Section 6).  The human
   researcher of this project (Koeplinger) participated in that
   discussion.

2. **Dixon (1995, 2005, 2010)**: Proposes octonionic descriptions of Λ
   in O³ (in the 2010 preprint written with the ordinary octonion
   product and derived from Wilson's; earlier versions in the 2005
   preprint and the 1995 arXiv preprints) and obtains the integral
   octonions from octonion X- and XY-products, but does not establish
   closure of Λ under any product.

3. **Wilson (2009)**: Characterizes Λ as a sublattice of L³ using
   octonionic conditions, but does not define or test a multiplicative
   product on Λ.

4. **Earlier precursors**: additive/geometric constructions of Λ
   that foreshadow the octonion-triple and Jordan-algebra frameworks
   above but do not test closure under any bilinear product:
   Conway–Sloane 1982 (23 constructions, one per Niemeier lattice,
   including a Turyn-style construction from three copies of E₈);
   Lepowsky–Meurman 1982 (Λ as a sublattice of E₈³ containing 2E₈³,
   with a maximal 2-local subgroup that permutes the three factors and
   one extra automorphism generating Co₀; their §2 constructs Λ from any
   pair of complementary maximal totally singular subspaces of E₈/2E₈,
   the form of Egan's construction, and their introduction credits work
   of Tits with what amounts to the same construction; paper Appendix A);
   and Elkies–Gross 1996 (an integer form of the exceptional Jordan
   algebra 𝔥₃(O) with a positive definite element of determinant 1
   outside the orbit of the identity matrix, related to Λ and to the
   even rank-26 lattice of determinant 3 and minimum 4).

## Detailed findings

### [ConwaySloane1982] — Twenty-three constructions for the Leech lattice

Conway and Sloane give 23 constructions of Λ, one for each of the 23
Niemeier lattices (equivalently, one for each class of deep hole in
the Leech lattice).  Each construction glues vectors according to
the hole structure.  Among the 23 is a Turyn-style construction from
three copies of E₈ corresponding to the 3E₈ Niemeier lattice; the later
"triple of octonions / E₈³" approaches (Dixon 1995/2010, Wilson 2009)
share its E₈³ setting.  Lepowsky and Meurman, from the same year, credit
work of Tits with what amounts to their construction of Λ from E₈³ (see
[LepowskyMeurman1982]).  An expanded version appears as Chapter 24 of SPLAG.
Published in Proc. Roy. Soc. London A 381 (1982), 275–283.  The 23
constructions are additive/code-theoretic; no bilinear product is
defined on Λ.

Direct PDF access was not available during this search (paywalled).
Structural content confirmed via Wilson 2009 (which cites this as
reference [3]) and secondary sources; physical copy to be
obtained from library for verification.

### [LepowskyMeurman1982] — An E₈-approach to the Leech lattice and the Conway group

Lepowsky and Meurman (J. Algebra 77 (1982), no. 2, 484–504;
doi:10.1016/0021-8693(82)90268-X) describe Λ as a sublattice of E₈³
containing 2E₈³ and use this to construct the Conway group Co₀ via a
maximal 2-local subgroup permuting the three E₈ factors together with one
extra automorphism.  Their §2, read in the original at prompt 326 (the
author's copy; the article is in Elsevier's open archive): for lattices M
with 2Γ < M < Γ, Γ = E₈, Proposition 2.1 shows that 2^{-1/2}M is even
unimodular exactly when M/2Γ is a maximal totally singular subspace of
Γ/2Γ; Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 build, from any two such lattices Φ, Ψ
with Φ + Ψ = Γ and Φ ∩ Ψ = 2Γ, the lattice
{(x₁+y, x₂+y, x₃+y) : xᵢ ∈ Φ, y ∈ Ψ, x₁+x₂+x₃ ∈ 2Γ}, and 2^{-1/2} times it
is isometric to the Leech lattice; Proposition 2.4: 2^{-1/2}Φ and
2^{-1/2}Ψ are isometric to E₈.  Their introduction credits work of Tits
with what amounts to the same construction.  Wilson's Λ is their lattice
for (Ls̄, Ls), and Egan's construction is of this form.  Their term is
"totally singular" (Q = 0); the paper says "totally isotropic" and
"Lagrangian".  The paper cites them in Section 6 (the precursors) and in
Appendix A ("Relation to prior work", their §2).  No bilinear product on
Λ is defined or tested.  The E₈³ ambient decomposition anticipates the
R²⁴ = O₁ ⊕ O₂ ⊕ O₃ viewpoint used here and in Wilson 2009 / Dixon 2010 /
Baez 2014, but the multiplicative question is not engaged.  Note: the
1982 paper is by Lepowsky and Meurman alone; Frenkel joined the
collaboration later for the Monster vertex operator algebra (FLM, 1988).

### [Dixon1995] — Octonions: invariant Leech lattice exposed

Dixon's 1995 arXiv preprint (hep-th/9506080, 12 June 1995; preceded by
the closely related hep-th/9504040, 7 April 1995, "Octonions:
invariant representation of the Leech lattice") proposes an early
octonionic description of Λ.  The two 1995 preprints are references [10]
and [11] of Wilson 2009, which counts them among the attempts to build
the Leech lattice from triples of integral octonions; the paper's
Section 6 footnote describes the 1995 preprint as a precursor of Dixon's
2010 construction.  The formula Wilson corrected is in the 2005 preprint
(see [Dixon2005]).  Dixon 1995 does not test or claim closure of Λ under
any bilinear product.

### [Dixon2005] — Integral octonions, octonion multiplication and the Leech lattice

Dixon's 2005 preprint (dated 10 March 2005), cited as reference [12] of
Wilson 2009.  Its conjectured formula for the third family of minimal
vectors required a small correction, which Wilson supplied in a bracketed
note in §5 of Wilson 2009 while independently deriving the sublattice
characterization above.  The Dixon–Wilson situation is structurally
analogous to the Kirmse–Bruck–Coxeter one: an early, structurally
correct proposal refined by a later short correction.  The preprint was
posted at www.7stones.com/Homepage/8Lattice.pdf; that URL now serves
Dixon's later 2010 paper, so the 2005 preprint is cited by its locator
through Wilson 2009 rather than by a live link.  Like Dixon's other works
here, it does not test or claim closure of Λ under a bilinear product.

### [ElkiesGross1996] — The exceptional cone and the Leech lattice

Elkies and Gross (Internat. Math. Res. Notices 1996, no. 14,
665–698) work in the 27-dimensional exceptional Jordan algebra
𝔥₃(O), on the lattice J of Hermitian 3×3 matrices with entries in
Coxeter's ring of integral octonions, with the cubic determinant form
d.  Aut(J, d) has two orbits on the positive definite P ∈ J with
d(P) = 1 (Gross, Invent. Math. 124 (1996), 263–279); they choose such
a polarization E from the orbit not containing the identity matrix.
Its Jordan root system has 819 elements, and they study the relation
between (J, d, E), the even lattice J₀ = (ZE)⊥ of rank 26,
determinant 3 and minimum 4 (144 × 819 minimal vectors), and Λ
(240 × 819 minimal vectors).  Sources: the zbMATH reviews of this
paper (Zbl 0863.11027) and of the 2001 follow-up below.  The rank of
J₀ is 26, so J₀ is not Λ.  The theorem that octave reflections
generate Aut K for two integral forms K of 𝔥₃(O) is Allcock's
("Reflection groups on the octave hyperbolic plane", J. Algebra 213
(1999), 467–498), on forms acting on the octave hyperbolic plane; it
is not part of this paper.

Wilson 2009 groups this with Dixon 1995 as one of the "tried to
build Leech from triples of integral octonions" attempts (Wilson's
reference [13]).  Structurally this is the nearest precursor to the
Baez/Egan 2014 Jordan-closure investigation: both work in 𝔥₃(O).
The Elkies–Gross treatment is additive/geometric (lattices and
their automorphism groups); the question of whether Λ or the ambient integer
form is closed under the (undoubled or doubled) Jordan product is
not raised.  A follow-up paper "Cubic rings and the exceptional
Jordan algebra" (Elkies–Gross, Duke Math. J. 109 (2001), 383–409)
further investigates these structures using modular forms and
Niemeier's classification.

Direct PDF access was not available during this search (paywalled);
structural content confirmed via Elkies' publication list, Baez's
Integral Octonions n-Category Café series, and Wilson 2009.
Physical copy to be obtained from library for verification.

### [Wilson2009] — Octonions and the Leech lattice

Wilson constructs Λ as {(x,y,z) ∈ L³ : conditions 1–3}, gives the
explicit decomposition 196,560 = 3 × 240 × (1 + 16 + 16²) of the
minimal shell into three families, and proves Λ is even and self-dual
by elementary arguments using L and the sublattices Ls, Ls̄.  Wilson's
paper does not describe the Conway group; it announces at the end
that a follow-on paper ("Conway's group and octonions", listed as
reference [20] of Wilson 2009 and submitted at the time) will
generate Aut(Λ) using 3×3 octonionic matrices.  See the [WilsonConway]
entry below for that follow-on.  Wilson 2009 does not define or test
a bilinear product on Λ; it uses the octonionic structure for the
sublattice characterization only.

### [Dixon2010] — Integral Octonions, Octonion XY-Product, and the Leech Lattice

Dixon's 2010 preprint (arXiv:1011.2541) obtains the integral octonions
from the octonion XY-product

    A ∘_{XY} B  =  (A X)(Y† B),

where X, Y ∈ O are chosen so that Y X† is a unit octonion (the
identity of the new multiplication).  The special case Y† = X⁻¹ gives
the X-product A ∘_X B = (A X)(X⁻¹ B).  Dixon uses the X-product with
X = ℓ₀⁻¹, where ℓ₀ = ½(1 + e₁ + … + e₇), to obtain E₈, and XY-products
for the laminated lattice Λ₁₆.  For Λ, Dixon writes the minimal vectors
of a copy of Λ (the componentwise conjugates of Wilson's, key claim 005)
in O³ with the ordinary octonion product.  Each XY-product is an
alternative octonion multiplication on R⁸ (isomorphic to the
standard one).  Dixon does not test or claim closure of Λ under the
XY-product or any other bilinear product.  The human researcher of this
project (Koeplinger) learned the name "X-product" from Dixon, who
attributes it to Martin Cederwall (prompt 331).  Dixon's preprint
"Octonion X-product orbits" ([Dixon1994], §1) credits the introduction
of the X-product to Cederwall and Preitschopf
([CederwallPreitschopf1995]), with X a unit octonion.  The paper
(Section 6, after display (6.1)) gives the X-product in the form
A ∘_X B = (AX)(X⁻¹B) and names no originator; none of its proofs uses
it.

### [Baez2014] — Integral Octonions, parts 9–12 (n-Category Café)

John Baez's blog series (parts 9–12, November 2014 to January 2016; the
series runs 2013–2016) explores the
exceptional Jordan algebra 𝔥₃(O) as a framework for the Leech lattice.
Part 9 (23 November 2014) gives Greg Egan's construction of the Leech
lattice from E₈ alone, in the form of part 11 (11 December 2014): for
sublattices L₁, L₂ ⊂ L, each isometric to √2 L, with L₁ ∩ L₂ = 2L, the
lattice Λ(L₁, L₂) := {(x,y,z) ∈ L³ : x+y, x+z, y+z ∈ L₁; x+y+z ∈ L₂} is
isometric to Λ.  Part 11 gives the 270 such sublattices (the rescaled
copies of the first E₈ shell); each meets exactly 64 of them in 2L, so the
construction gives 17,280 = 270 × 64 lattices, all distinct (Egan's
comments, [Egan2014]).  Wilson's Λ = Λ(Ls̄, Ls) and σ(Λ) = Λ(2𝒪, p𝒪) are
two of them (paper Section 6, display (6.2)).  Parts 9–10 (part 10:
1 December 2014) contain the Leech–Jordan material, and part 12
(31 January 2016, by Tim Silverman) the integral octonions modulo 2.  The
paper cites parts 9 and 11 for Egan's construction and its count and parts
9–10 for the Jordan material (Section 6), and part 12 for Silverman's
identification (Appendix A).  Links:
https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2014/11/integral_octonions_part_9.html,
https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2014/12/integral_octonions_part_10.html,
https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2014/12/integral_octonions_part_11.html,
https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2016/01/integral_octonions_part_12.html.
Baez considers the 27-dimensional lattice ℤ³ ⊕ L_L ⊂ 𝔥₃(O) with integers
on the diagonal and, off the diagonal, a Leech lattice L_L at the standard
scale with entries in a maximal order of integral octonions (L_L ≅ Λ/√2).
Key findings:

- Egan found [Egan2014] that the lattice ℤ³ ⊕ L_L ⊂ 𝔥₃(O) is NOT closed
  under the Jordan product x ∘ y = xy + yx.
- It IS closed under the doubled Jordan product 2(xy + yx).
- Egan enumerated at least 17,280 distinct off-diagonal Leech lattices
  L_L ⊂ O³ for which the associated 27-dimensional lattice is closed
  under the doubled product (part 10, its first Result 1): those of
  Egan's construction with a maximal order in place of L, for which the count is
  exact (part 10's second Result 1; part 11).  Part 10 asks (Puzzle 3)
  whether there are more.  Egan's orbit-stabilizer bound gives at least
  244,035,421 sublattices of L³ isometric to Λ (paper Section 6).
- Baez left as an open question whether any such lattice is closed
  under the undoubled Jordan product xy + yx, which would remove the
  factor-of-2 discrepancy, and conjectured that the answer is
  negative ("I suspect the answer is no!").
- Baez expresses dissatisfaction with the factor of 2: "the factor of 2
  in condition c) really annoys me."
- **The human researcher (Koeplinger) is quoted in the discussion**:
  "I ran several kinds of (exceptional) Jordan products through my little
  program...but essentially didn't find anything that would close on the
  Leech inner shell."

This is the closest prior art to our finding, but uses a fundamentally
different algebraic framework (commutative Jordan product in 27
dimensions) rather than a noncommutative, nonassociative
octonion-derived product in 24 dimensions.

### [Calderbank2023] — Egan's construction and Lepowsky–Meurman (comment on Baez, part 9)

David Calderbank, in a comment of 2 April 2023 on part 9 of Baez's
Integral Octonions series
(https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2014/11/integral_octonions_part_9.html#c062182),
relates Egan's construction to the earlier literature.  Calderbank
credits Baez and Egan with noting that the octonions are not needed ("the
octonionic yoga is not really necessary"): the ingredients are the
preimages in L of two complementary maximal isotropic subspaces of L/2L,
and the triples (x+a, x+b, x+c) built from them give the Leech lattice.
The comment names Tits (1980) and Lepowsky and Meurman (1982) among those
who knew this "Turyn-inspired" construction from three copies of E₈
(Calderbank has seen only Lepowsky and Meurman's paper, which quotes
Tits), notes that it "isn't quite the Turyn construction", and relates it
to the 23 constructions of Conway and Sloane and to Borcherds's thesis.
The comment gives no theorem number.  The paper's Appendix A credits
Calderbank for relating Egan's construction to that of Lepowsky and
Meurman; it credits the identification itself to Lepowsky and Meurman
(§2), and calls the orthogonal space (L/2L, q) classical (Wilson's book,
§3.12.4).  This project arrived at the complementary-pair reading
independently (prompt-log record).

### [Egan2014] — Comments on Baez, parts 9 and 11 (n-Category Café)

Greg Egan's comments of 26 November 2014 on part 9
(https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2014/11/integral_octonions_part_9.html#c047749)
and of 11 and 13 December 2014 on part 11
(https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2014/12/integral_octonions_part_11.html#c047862
and #c047873).  The comment of 26 November finds that the
27-dimensional lattice Z³ ⊕ L_L of Hermitian matrices is not closed under
the Jordan product xy + yx but is closed under the doubled product
2(xy + yx).  The comment of 11 December shows that a Leech lattice built
by Egan's construction determines its pair (L₁, L₂), so the
construction's 17,280 lattices are distinct; the comment of 13 December
counts 64 complementary partners for each of the 270 sublattices,
270 × 64 = 17,280 ordered pairs.  The paper cites these
comments in Section 6, for the count and for the Jordan closure.  Blog
comments, not peer-reviewed; no dispute known.

### [Silverman2016] — Comments on Baez, part 12 (n-Category Café)

Tim Silverman's comments of 16 and 19 February 2016 on part 12 of Baez's
series
(https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2016/01/integral_octonions_part_12.html#c050266
and #c050282).  The comment of 16 February reads Egan's construction
modulo 2, with the parametrization (x+p, x+q, x+r); the comment of
19 February translates Wilson's octonionic construction down to F₂ and
finds that Wilson's Ls̄ and Ls reduce to two disjoint maximal isotropic
subspaces, that is, a polarization.  Part 12 itself (31 January 2016, by
Silverman) identifies the 270 sublattices of E₈ isometric to √2 E₈ with
the maximal totally isotropic subspaces of E₈/2E₈, independently of
Lepowsky and Meurman, and says that this was assumed, not proved.  The
paper cites the comments in Appendix A ("Relation to prior work"), at the
author's direction (prompt 327): the online discussion gave the research a
key impulse.  Blog comments, not peer-reviewed; no dispute
known.

### [WilsonConway] — Conway's group and octonions

Wilson's follow-on to Wilson 2009 describes Conway's group Co₀,
the double cover of Co₁, using right multiplications by 3×3 octonionic
matrices.  This uses the octonion product to describe symmetries, not
to define a closed algebra on Λ.

## Thematically adjacent — order on E₈, not on Λ

### [Corradetti2026]

- **Authors**: Daniele Corradetti
- **Title**: Integral elements of Okubo algebra and the E₈-lattice
- **Published in**: arXiv:2605.09333 (v1, 10 May 2026; v2, 19 August 2026)
- **Year**: 2026
- **URL / DOI**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09333
- **Topics**: octonions, okubo_algebra, e8_lattice, orders
- **Originality note**: arXiv preprint; no dispute known.
- **Notes**: The current version (v2, 19 August 2026) introduces an
  integral order-three automorphism of the octonions whose Petersson isotope
  is the compact real Okubo algebra, and shows that the Coxeter–Dickson E₈
  lattice carries octonionic, para-octonionic and Okubo integral structures,
  three multiplicative systems that are not isomorphic; the four octonionic
  orders with lattices C₈, A₂⁴, D₄² and E₈ are preserved by the new
  realization of the Okubo product.  The paper (§6, "Related work")
  describes this version.  v1 (10 May 2026) took a 2-adic route instead (a
  Z[√3]-order after 2-adic scaling, E₈ recovered by 2-adic saturation); the
  analysis note `paper/reviews/2026-05-22_claude_opus_4_7_corradetti_analysis.md`
  is on v1.

### [Koeplinger2026] — An order on the Leech lattice from a Z₃-symmetric triple-octonion product

This project's own result, recorded here because the registry is exhaustive
of works cited in this repository.

J. Köplinger, "An order on the Leech lattice from a Z₃-symmetric
triple-octonion product", 2026, doi:10.13140/RG.2.2.22093.19686
(ResearchGate).  The Leech lattice Λ is closed under a Z₃-symmetric
triple-octonion product on R²⁴; in Wilson's sublattice description of Λ,
closure turns on a transposition σ of two imaginary basis units.  The
corresponding source is frozen in this repository as
`paper/main_2026-07-19.tex` (v7, 24 pages).  Originality note: the
construction and its verification are original to this project; the prior
art it builds on and is contrasted with is registered above.
