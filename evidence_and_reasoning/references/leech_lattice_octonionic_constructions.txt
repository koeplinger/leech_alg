Topic: Octonionic constructions of the Leech lattice
=========================================================

Created: 2026-04-06.  Updated: 2026-10-24.

This file collects references for works that construct, or contribute to constructing,
the Leech lattice using octonions, the E8 lattice, and related structures.

---

### [Wilson2009]

- **Authors**: Robert A. Wilson
- **Title**: Octonions and the Leech lattice
- **Published in**: Journal of Algebra, Volume 322, Issue 7, 2009, pages 2186–2190
- **Year**: 2009
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2009.03.021
- **Article history**: Received 18 December 2008; available online 27 March 2009;
  communicated by Gernot Stroth
- **Affiliation**: School of Mathematical Sciences, Queen Mary, University of London,
  Mile End Road, London E1 4NS, United Kingdom
- **Source**: Not redistributed locally (Elsevier publisher copyright).
  Canonical DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2009.03.021
- **Topics**: Leech lattice, octonions, E8 lattice, octonionic Leech lattice,
  Coxeter–Dickson nonassociative ring, minimal vectors, Conway group
- **Originality note**: Published in a peer-reviewed journal (Journal of Algebra,
  Elsevier). Wilson claims this construction is "new" and provides "the first real
  explanation" of why the minimal vector count 196560 factors as
  3 × 240 × (1 + 16 + 16 × 16). The paper explicitly situates itself relative to
  prior constructions (referencing 23 other constructions cited in [3] and 4
  constructions in [16] of Wilson's bibliography). No dispute over priority or
  originality is known to this project at time of writing. If a dispute is
  discovered later it must be added here.
- **Notes**: The construction defines the octonionic Leech lattice Λ as the set of
  triples (x, y, z) of octonions from the E8 lattice L, subject to three
  conditions (Definition 2.1 of the paper). In Wilson's notation L = (1 + i_0)A,
  where A = ½(1 + i_0)L is "a copy of the Coxeter–Dickson integral octonions"
  (p. 2187); L itself is a nonunital order and, as a lattice, a √2-scaled copy
  of a maximal order of the integral octonions (paper Section 2.2). That
  section fixes one such order, 𝒪 = ½(e_1 + e_2)L (display (2.2)), and
  identifies it with Wilson's ring A_3 = ½(1 + i_3)B(1 + i_3) of his Section 2,
  where B = ½Ls̄ and i_t = e_{t+1} (checked in
  python_project/src/verify_structural_proof.py). The three-coordinate
  structure Λ ⊂ L³ (Wilson's Section 3, cited in paper Section 1) is the
  setting of the paper's construction (paper Section 3).

### [Dixon2010]

- **Authors**: Geoffrey Dixon
- **Title**: Integral Octonions, Octonion XY-Product, and the Leech Lattice
- **Published in**: arXiv preprint (no journal publication). Author
  affiliation listed as www.7stones.com (Dixon's personal website).
- **Year**: 2010 (document dated November 21, 2010)
- **arXiv ID**: 1011.2541 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.2541
- **Source**: Canonical preprint hosted on arXiv under arXiv's
  non-exclusive distribution license. Not separately redistributed
  from this repository.
- **Topics**: Leech lattice, integral octonions, XY-product, X-product, E8 lattice,
  laminated lattices in dimensions 4, 8, 16, 24
- **Originality note**: This is an unpublished preprint and has not undergone formal
  peer review. The XY-product is Dixon's own earlier work; no dispute over that is
  known to this project. Its special case, the X-product, was introduced, per Dixon
  ([Dixon1994] in background_octonions.txt, §1), by Cederwall and Preitschopf
  ([CederwallPreitschopf1995], ibid.).
  Because the document has not been peer-reviewed, independent verification of its
  claims is required before they are relied upon in this project. No plagiarism
  dispute is known at time of writing.
- **Notes**: Dixon constructs representations of the E8 lattice (Λ8) using the
  X-product variant of octonion multiplication, and extends this to reach the
  24-dimensional Leech lattice via a construction involving three copies. The
  XY-product (A ∘_XY B := (AX)(Y†B)) is central to the paper and differs from
  standard octonion multiplication. This approach is a second independent line of
  evidence for the three-copies-of-E8 structure on which this project's
  Z₃-symmetric triple-octonion product ⋆ is built. Section 6 of the project's
  paper describes the XY-product (display (6.1)) and the X-product and reports an
  independent reproduction of Dixon's construction (python_project/src/leech_dixon.py,
  e8_dixon.py).

### [WilsonBook2009]

- **Authors**: Robert A. Wilson
- **Title**: The Finite Simple Groups
- **Published in**: Graduate Texts in Mathematics, Volume 251, Springer, London, 2009
- **Year**: 2009
- **URL / DOI**: 10.1007/978-1-84800-988-2 (ISBN 978-1-84800-987-5 print,
  978-1-84800-988-2 electronic)
- **Topics**: E8 lattice, Leech lattice, background (the orthogonal space E8/2E8)
- **Originality note**: Standard textbook; no dispute known.
- **Notes**: Cited twice in the paper's Appendix A, both at §3.12.4 (p. 104): the
  Weyl group of E8, the isometry group of L, acts on L/2L as the full orthogonal
  group of q, with kernel {±1} ("Which polarizations close"); and the orthogonal
  space (L/2L, q) is classical ("Relation to prior work"). Read at prompt 325 in the
  publisher's preview and in Wilson's draft of 23 July 2007. Fields checked against
  Crossref at prompt 334 (the Crossref record omits the volume number).
