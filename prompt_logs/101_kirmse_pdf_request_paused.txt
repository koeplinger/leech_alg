Prompt 101 — Request for a self-contained Kirmse PDF; paused on a framing conflict
Date: 2026-05-20

User request (verbatim, abridged):
"This is an enormous finding. Remember when we read Wikipedia's (unverified)
claim that Kirmse did indeed find 7 (but not 8) integral orders? ... Make a
self-contained PDF write-up on Kirmse's paper alone ... showing all of
Kirmse's definitions and derivations that lead to the conclusion that he did
indeed find 7 (but not 8) integral orders ... make yourself the author ...
reference the prompt that led you there ... wait on the Coxeter paper before
finalizing."

Why this prompt was PAUSED (manifesto sec. 4 uncertainty protocol, sec. 11
forward-evolving corrections apply to human framing too):

The requested PDF thesis -- "Kirmse did indeed find 7 (but not 8) integral
orders" -- is NOT supported by the verification done in Prompt 100, and the
one checkable data point contradicts it:
  - Kirmse CLAIMED eight maximal orders ("Man findet acht solche", p.70).
  - He exhibited explicitly exactly ONE order, J_1.
  - Verified from scratch: J_1 is NOT closed -> not one of the 7 genuine
    maximal orders.
  - He never listed the other seven of his "eight"; only a schematic form
    is given. So "Kirmse found the seven genuine orders" cannot be verified
    from his paper, and the single order he did write down was a wrong one.
The verification therefore CONFIRMS the traditional "Kirmse's mistake"
(Petersson) and does NOT support Wikipedia's "the seven ... were constructed
by Kirmse (1924), Dickson and Bruck."

Action: did NOT write the PDF. Flagged the discrepancy to the user and
proposed an accurate thesis for the write-up; awaiting an explicit decision
before proceeding.
