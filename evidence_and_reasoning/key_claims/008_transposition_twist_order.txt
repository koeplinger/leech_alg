Claim number: 008
Title: The Z₃-symmetric triple-octonion product is an order on the Leech lattice Λ
Status: ESTABLISHED (symbolic proof + independent computational verification)
Created: 2026-04-12.  Updated: 2026-10-24.

--- RELATION TO CLAIM 007 ---

Claim 007 rules out the *untwisted* triple-octonion algebra, together with
the discrete and continuous modifications tested there, as an order on Λ.
Claim 008 is not a revision of 007: claim 007 holds for the family it
covers.  Claim 008 records a positive finding obtained by leaving that
family — specifically, by twisting the single octonion multiplication used
in all three blocks via a transposition of imaginary basis elements, a
construction not included in the discrete variants exhausted under claim
007.

--- STATEMENT ---

Let σ be a transposition of two imaginary basis elements of the
octonion algebra O (e.g. σ = (e₁ e₂)).  Define a new bilinear
multiplication on R⁸ by

    x ·_σ y := σ( σ(x) · σ(y) ),

where · is the standard octonion product.  Equivalently, σ : (O, ·)
→ (O, ·_σ) is an isomorphism of algebras.  σ preserves the Wilson E8
lattice L = D₈⁺: σ(L) = L (paper Lemma 4.1, `lem:sigma-L`), since a
coordinate permutation preserves the integer or half-integer type and the
parity of the coordinate sum.  σ moves the sublattices Ls and Ls̄ of L,
and that is where the twist does its work.

Define the Z₃-symmetric triple-octonion product on
R²⁴ = O₁ ⊕ O₂ ⊕ O₃ by using ·_σ in all three blocks with Z₃-symmetric
cross-block routing (same-block Oα × Oα → Oα; cross-block Oα × Oβ → Oγ
for {α, β, γ} = {1, 2, 3}).  It is an octonion product in all three
blocks; the transposition σ is the device that exhibits its fit with
Wilson's representation, not a new algebra.

Then the Leech lattice Λ (in Wilson's E8-triple realisation) is closed
under this product.  Consequently (Λ, +, ·_σ-triple) is an order in
the real non-associative R-algebra (R²⁴, +, ·_σ-triple) (paper Theorem 1.1
and Corollary 1.2, `thm:main` and `cor:order`).

--- PROOF ---

The symbolic proof is written out in full in paper/main.tex, Section 4
(`sec:proof`).  It reduces closure on Λ to four lemmas (paper Section 4.1),
which paper Propositions 4.6 to 4.8 (`prop:cond1` to `prop:cond3`) carry to
Wilson's three conditions:

  Lemma 4.1:  σ(L) = L          (`lem:sigma-L`; parity argument)
  Lemma 4.2:  L · L ⊆ L         (`lem:L-order`)
  Lemma 4.3:  L · σ(Ls̄) ⊆ σ(Ls̄)   (`lem:L-sigmaLsbar`)
              (σ(Ls̄) is a left L-module under the standard product)
  Lemma 4.4:  σ(Ls) · σ(Ls) ⊆ σ(Ls)   (`lem:sigmaLs-closed`)
              (σ(Ls) is closed under the standard product)

L does not contain e₀: it is a nonunital order and, as a lattice, a
√2-scaled copy of a maximal order of integral octonions (paper Section 2.2).
Lemmas 4.2 to 4.4 follow from the closure of one maximal order,
𝒪 := ½(e₁ + e₂)L (paper (2.2), `eq:MO`), with L = (e₁ + e₂)𝒪, and from three
facts: the lattice identities σ(Ls̄) = 2𝒪 and σ(Ls) = p𝒪 with
p = e₂ − e₄ + e₅ − e₆ (paper (4.1), `eq:multiples`); the identity
(xa)(xb) = x((ax)b) for purely imaginary x (paper (4.2),
`eq:moufang-imaginary`); and its consequence (xM)(xM) ⊆ xM for a closed
lattice M containing a purely imaginary x (paper (4.3),
`eq:multiple-closed`).  Ledger claims 019, 021 and 022.

σ(Ls) ≠ Ls, so the twist does nontrivial work; this is paper Remark 4.5
(`rem:Ls-not-closed`), not a step of the closure proof.

The core structural point: v ·_σ w, expanded via the bilinearity
decomposition, reduces each of Wilson's three sublattice conditions to
a statement about standard octonion products of the σ-images of the
input blocks.  Wilson's condition (3) becomes σ(Ls) · σ(Ls) ⊆ σ(Ls),
which holds (Lemma 4.4); the analogous statement for Ls itself is
*false* for the untwisted product — this is precisely the condition-3
failure recorded in claim 007.

The finite content of the proof is the closure of 𝒪 on a Z-basis and the
identities σ(Ls̄) = 2𝒪 and σ(Ls) = p𝒪 on eight products each
(python_project/src/verify_structural_proof.py).  The checks of Lemmas 4.2
to 4.4 on basis pairs, 64 products per lemma, are kept as validation (paper
Appendix C, `sec:methodology`); they are discharged by exact rational
arithmetic.

--- WHAT DOES AND DOES NOT FOLLOW ---

Follows:
 - (Λ, +, ·_σ-triple) is an order in the real R-algebra (R²⁴, +, ·_σ-triple).
 - The obstruction identified in claim 007 (failure of Wilson condition
   (3) for type3 × type3) is specific to the *untwisted* standard
   product in that block structure; a single transposition twist
   removes it.

Does NOT follow from closure (recorded elsewhere; ⋆ is the paper's name for
the ·_σ-triple product):
 - Any claim about associativity, alternativity, flexibility, power-
   associativity, unitality, or norm multiplicativity.  All of them fail on
   the order (paper Section 5.1, `sec:identities`): it is nonunital,
   noncommutative and not norm-multiplicative, and the ambient algebra
   (R²⁴, +, ⋆) has no identity element (paper Remark 5.2,
   `rem:no-identity`).  The ambient algebra has exactly eight idempotents,
   and none of the seven nonzero ones lies in Λ (e₀ ∉ L), so the order has
   no nonzero idempotent (paper Section 5.2, `sec:idempotents`; ledger
   claim 011).  Λ contains square-zero elements: those of norm 12 are
   exactly 4,032, every square-zero norm of Λ lies in 12Z, and norm 24
   occurs (paper Section 5.5, `sec:squarezero`; ledger claim 012).  Min(Λ)
   contains no idempotent and no square-zero vector (ledger claim 012a); an
   unscoped claim that the order has no nilpotents is false.
 - Maximality: the order is not maximal.  L³ is ⋆-closed, an even integral
   overlattice of Λ of index 2¹²; Λ + Zε₁ with ε₁ = (e₀, 0, 0) is a second
   ⋆-closed overlattice, of index 4, not contained in L³ and not integral
   (paper Section 5.3, `sec:maximality`; ledger claim 016).
 - The automorphism group: Aut(Λ, +, ⋆) is finite of order 36, with
   structure C₆ × S₃, and strictly contained in Co₀ (paper Section 5.4,
   `sec:autgroup`; ledger claims 010 and 010a; the companion note
   paper/automorphism_group_2026-07-12.tex).
 - Uniqueness up to isomorphism: twisted products with different span
   indices give orders on Λ that are not isomorphic, among them ⋆ (span
   index 2¹⁶), ⋆_π for a closing even permutation π (span index 2¹⁰) and
   ⋆_ρ for the reflection ρ (span index 1) (paper Section 5.6,
   `sec:census`; ledger claims 015 and 017).
