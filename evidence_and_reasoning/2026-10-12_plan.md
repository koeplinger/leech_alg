### (5) Section 5.2: cut the redundant clause; add that rescaling does not rescue an idempotent

### (6) Section 5.3: order versus ambient algebra; display the $C_6$ generator; closing sentence

  note.") into its own paragraph, since it applies to both the order and the

*What the record says about the sentence.*  It was added for review finding
M2 (prompts 245, 260, 261): the displayed generator preserves the twisted
product $\cdot_\sigma$ and does **not** preserve the standard product of
Section 2.1, so a reader who checks it against the Fano table finds a
non-automorphism unless told which product is meant.  The clause "every
automorphism is a blockwise octonion automorphism followed by a permutation
of the three blocks" is the part the second paragraph restates; the clause
scoping the generator to $(\mathbb{O}, \cdot_\sigma)$ is load-bearing.
Recommendation: drop the sentence and attach the scoping to the generator
instead, e.g. "the $C_6$ is generated by the following automorphism of
$(\mathbb{O}, \cdot_\sigma)$ (Definition 3.2), applied in each block, with
$e_0$ fixed:", followed by the three equations.

*The three equations.*  Following the displayed map through its orbits:
1. $e_1 \mapsto -e_5 \mapsto -e_6 \mapsto -e_1$, i.e. $e_1 \mapsto -e_5$,
   $e_5 \mapsto e_6$, $e_6 \mapsto e_1$: the cube of the map is $-1$ on this
   orbit, which is what gives the generator order 6;
2. $e_2 \mapsto -e_7 \mapsto e_3 \mapsto e_2$, i.e. $e_2 \mapsto -e_7$,
   $e_7 \mapsto -e_3$, $e_3 \mapsto e_2$: order 3 on this orbit;
3. $e_4 \mapsto -e_4$.

The author calls both cycle lines "$C_3$"; as permutations of the index sets
$\{1,5,6\}$ and $\{2,3,7\}$ they are 3-cycles, and the signs on the first make
it an element of order 6.  Second-pass verification of the orbit structure
and of the twisted-versus-standard preservation is in Section 4.

*Flag on the figure.*  The author writes "from $2^{24}$ (Baez--Egan)".  The
paper's figure for the Baez--Egan projected product $\varphi$ is $2^{25}$
(Section 6, "Anatomy of the Baez--Egan closure";

comparison value the paper uses to say that the span of $\varphi$ does not
contain $2\Lambda$.  Both scripts were re-run at prompt 281 and print $2^{25}$
and $2^{16}$ (Section 4).  Note for the wording: compared like for like
modulo $2\Lambda$, the $\star$-products span an 8-dimensional subspace of
$\Lambda/2\Lambda$ and the $\varphi$-products a 1-dimensional one, so the
raw indices compare products of different normalization at face value, as
Section 6 already does.  See Section 2, question (b).

*The witness, checked by hand.*  With $u = \varepsilon_1 = (e_0, 0, 0)$ and
$v = (x', y', z')$, the three displayed blocks of Definition 3.3 give
$P_1 = e_0 \cdot_\sigma x' = x'$, $P_2 = e_0 \cdot_\sigma z' = z'$, $P_3 = e_0
\cdot_\sigma y' = y'$, since $\sigma$ fixes $e_0$ and $e_0$ is the identity of
$\cdot_\sigma$; with the roles exchanged, $v \star \varepsilon_1$ gives the
same $(x', z', y')$.  Wilson's three conditions are symmetric in the three
blocks, so the swap preserves $\Lambda$, and $\varepsilon_1 \star
\varepsilon_1 = (e_0 \cdot_\sigma e_0, 0, 0) = \varepsilon_1$.  Hence
$(\Lambda + \mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_1) \star (\Lambda + \mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_1)
\subseteq \Lambda + \mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_1$; the index is
$[\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_1 : \Lambda \cap \mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_1] =
[\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_1 : 4\mathbb{Z}\varepsilon_1] = 4$; and
$\varepsilon_1 \notin L^3$ because $e_0$ has odd norm while $L$ is even.  The
overlattice lies in $\tfrac14\Lambda$ and is discrete.  Second-pass machine
verification is in Section 4.  When this item is executed, the maximality
entry of the open-question lists in `CURRENT_STATE.md` and `README.md`
follows, and the witness gets a row in the key-claims ledger.
