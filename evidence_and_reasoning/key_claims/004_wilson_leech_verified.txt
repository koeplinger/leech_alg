Claim number: 004
Title: Wilson's octonionic construction yields the 196,560 minimal vectors of the Leech lattice
Status: ESTABLISHED
Created: 2026-04-07.  Updated: 2026-10-28.

--- STATEMENT ---

Wilson [Wilson2009 Section 3] defines the Leech lattice as:

    Λ = { (x, y, z) ∈ L³ :
          (1)  x, y, z ∈ L
          (2)  x+y, x+z, y+z ∈ Ls̄
          (3)  x+y+z ∈ Ls }

where L is the E8 lattice, s = ½(−e₀+e₁+...+e₇) (WILSON_S),
s̄ = conjugate of s, and:

    Ls̄ = {λ × s̄ : λ ∈ L}   (index-16 sublattice of L, = 2B)
    Ls  = {λ × s  : λ ∈ L}   (index-16 sublattice of L)

The 196,560 minimal vectors of Λ (standard R^24 norm-squared = 8) fall into
three families:

    Type 1:  (2λ, 0, 0) + 2 cyclic perms          — 3 × 240      =    720
    Type 2:  (λs̄, (λs̄)j, 0) + 2 cyclic perms     — 3 × 240 × 16 = 11,520
    Type 3:  ((λs)j, λk, (λj)k) + 2 cyclic perms  — 3 × 240 × 16 × 16 = 184,320

    Total: 196,560 = 3 × 240 × (1 + 16 + 256)

where λ runs over the 240 E8 roots and j, k ∈ J = {±e_t : t = 0,...,7}.

CRITICAL NOTE ON TYPE-3 FORMULA:
Type-2 uses s̄ in BOTH components.
Type-3 uses s (not s̄) in the FIRST component.
Using s̄ in type-3 produces 184,320 norm-8 triples that do NOT satisfy
Wilson's membership conditions: each fails conditions (2) and (3)
(python_project/src/consistency_checks.py, check 9f, finds one such triple
outside Λ).  Wilson's bracketed note on the conjectured formula of Dixon's
2005 preprint (reference [12] of [Wilson2009]; not the 2010 construction of
claim 005) [Wilson2009 Section 5, p. 2189] concerns a different point, the
multiplication by j in the first coordinate:
  "in effect, his formula is equivalent to (λs, ±λk, ±(λj)k), that is,
   omitting the (necessary) multiplication by j in the first coordinate."

All three families satisfy conditions (1)–(3) above [Wilson2009 Section 3];
test_leech_wilson.py checks type 1 in full and types 2 and 3 on samples of
about 500.

--- SUBLATTICE STRUCTURES ---

Ls̄ = 2B:
  Z-basis: images of an L Z-basis under right-mult by s̄.
  Gram det: 256 (index [L : Ls̄] = 16, so [L : Ls̄]² × det(Gram_L) = 256).
  Minimum norm: N(λ) × N(s̄) = 2 × 2 = 4 (no norm-2 elements, so no E8 roots).

Ls:
  Z-basis: images of an L Z-basis under right-mult by s.
  Gram det: 256 (index [L : Ls] = 16, same argument).
  Minimum norm: 4.

Key relations:
  Ls̄ ∩ Ls = 2L  (all 2×root are in both; minimum norm = 8)
  Ls̄ + Ls = L   (Wilson Section 2; the combined generators have rank 8)
  2L ⊆ Ls̄ and 2L ⊆ Ls

--- NORM AND COUNT VERIFICATION ---

Type-1: N_std(2λ, 0, 0) = 4 N(λ) = 4 × 2 = 8. Count: 3 × 240 = 720. ✓
Type-2: N_std(λs̄, (λs̄)j, 0) = N(λ)N(s̄) + N(λ)N(s̄)N(j) = 4 + 4 = 8.
        Count: 3 × 240 × 16 = 11,520. ✓
Type-3: N_std((λs)j, λk, (λj)k) = N(λ)N(s)N(j) + N(λ)N(k) + N(λ)N(j)N(k)
                                  = 4 + 2 + 2 = 8.
        Count: 3 × 240 × 16 × 16 = 184,320. ✓
Total: 720 + 11,520 + 184,320 = 196,560. ✓

--- LATTICE PROPERTIES ---

Rank 24: the 196,560 minimal vectors span R^24.

Even lattice: every N_std lies in 4Z (Wilson's argument below), so Wilson's
norms are even; checked exactly on a Z-basis of Λ by
python_project/src/verify_square_zero_classification.py (diagonal Gram
entries in 4Z, the others in 2Z).  test_even_lattice_type1_gram checks the
720×720 Gram matrix of the type-1 vectors (all entries in 2Z); these span
only (2L)³.

Self-dual (det_Wilson = 1): proved analytically in [Wilson2009 Section 4].
Under the standard R^24 Euclidean inner product, det(Gram_Λ) = 2^24 (because
Wilson's norm is ½ of the standard norm, scaling the Gram det by 2^24).

No norm-2 vectors (Wilson's argument, [Wilson2009 Section 4, p. 2189]):
  For (x, y, z) ∈ Λ, N_std(x, y, z) = N(x+y) + N(x+z) + N(y+z) − N(x+y+z).
  The terms on the right are norms of elements of Ls̄ and Ls, and
  N(l s̄) = N(l s) = 2N(l) for l ∈ L, which lies in 4Z and is at least 4 for
  l ≠ 0; so N_std ∈ 4Z.  A nonzero vector with N_std < 8 therefore has
  N_std = 4, so one coordinate is 0 (three nonzero ones give N_std ≥ 6), say
  z = 0.  Then x and y lie in Ls̄, so only one of them is nonzero (two would
  give N_std ≥ 8), say x; then x = x+y+z ∈ Ls as well, so x ∈ Ls̄ ∩ Ls = 2L
  and N(x) ≥ 8, a contradiction.
  Therefore min norm = 8 (minimum is achieved by the 196,560 minimal vectors).

These four properties uniquely characterize the Leech lattice by Conway's
theorem [Conway–Sloane, Chapter 12].

--- ANSWER TO PROMPT 006 ---

The user asked: "does Wilson's construction yield a multiplication that closes
on the Leech lattice?"

Precise answer: Wilson does NOT define a "multiplication that closes on the
Leech lattice."  He constructs the Leech lattice as a SET Λ ⊂ L³ via three
algebraic conditions, then characterizes its 196,560 minimal vectors.

The object Wilson constructs IS the Leech lattice (by the four
properties under LATTICE PROPERTIES; test_leech_wilson.py checks conditions 1–3
on all 720 type-1 vectors and on samples of about 500 type-2 and 500 type-3
vectors).

The computational tools built here — the membership test is_in_leech(x, y, z)
and the three minimal-vector generators — are the reference for evaluating
whether a proposed product maps E8 elements into the Leech lattice.
