Work done:
  - Re-read Kirmse's section 2 (pp.68-71) forensically. The eight orders are
    discussed ONLY on p.70: the assertion "Man findet acht solche", the
    schematic form [J_0, 1/2(quadruple), 1/2 sum i], the structural remark
    "14 middle elements in 7 complementary pairs", and exactly ONE explicit
    order, J_1. No computation is printed; the schematic, read as Kirmse's
    module-bracket (defined p.68), spans only an index-<=4 module and so
    cannot by itself construct a maximal order. => the explicit text does
    NOT construct the eight.
  - Forensic computation (python_project/src/verify_kirmse_1924_forensic.py),
    Kirmse's table (1) only:
      * 30 E8-type candidate lattices over J_0;
      * exactly 7 closed under multiplication (genuine maximal orders);
      * exactly 8 invariant under multiplication by the UNITS;
      * the 8 unit-invariant lattices = the 7 genuine orders + Kirmse's J_1;
      * J_1 is the unique unit-invariant non-order;
      * J_1's closure failures are confined to the half x half products
        (10 of 16 of a_i*a_j fail; all 128 products involving a unit close).
  - Interpretation (reconstruction, since Kirmse printed no computation):
    Kirmse's "einfache endliche Rechnung" most plausibly tested invariance
    under the units -- a necessary but NOT sufficient closure condition --
    which yields exactly 8; the missing condition is closure under products
    of two half-integer elements; the 8th, defective lattice is precisely
    his J_1. Three independent matches (count 8; contains the 7; J_1 = the
    bad one) make this strongly supported, though it remains an inference.
  - Consequence: Kirmse's enumeration was of unit-invariant lattices, not of
    orders; his paper does not establish him as having identified/verified
    the seven. Recorded in
    paper/reviews/2026-05-20_claude_opus_4_7_kirmse_forensic.md.
