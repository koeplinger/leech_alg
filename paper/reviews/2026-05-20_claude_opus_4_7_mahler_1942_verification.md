## What Mahler did

Mahler builds the octonions by Cayley–Dickson doubling (his product equals
Dickson's), takes the **integral Cayley numbers "defined according to
Dickson"** — citing Dickson 1923, p. 319 ff. — as a ring $J$, and proves:

- **Theorem 1 (Euclidean algorithm).** To every C-number $X$ there is an
  integral $G$ with $N(X-G)\le \tfrac{15}{16}<1$. Mahler adds, candidly,
  that $\tfrac{15}{16}$ "is not the best possible … the exact constant is
  $\tfrac12$," omitting that sharper proof as unnecessary.
- **Theorem 2.** Every left ideal of $J$ is principal, generated by
  $G_0=\delta G^*$ with $\delta$ a rational integer and $G^*$ of norm
  $1$, $2$, or $4$. Mahler states explicitly that the converse fails — *not*
  every such $G_0$ generates an ideal — and gives a concrete counterexample.

## Verification from scratch

1. **The product is a genuine octonion algebra**, identical to Dickson's
   Cayley–Dickson product.
2. **Mahler's ring $J$ is a genuine maximal order**, and equals Dickson's
   order $O_D$: his membership criterion (14) and his basis (10) define the
   same index-16 lattice, it is closed under multiplication, and it
   coincides with Dickson's system (30). It has **240** elements of norm 1
   and **2160** of norm 2 — exactly as Mahler states.
3. **Theorem 1.** The covering radius² of $J$ is **$\tfrac12$** (an explicit
   deep hole at depth² $\tfrac12$ was exhibited; 6000 samples never exceed
   it). This confirms both Mahler's *proved* bound ($\tfrac12\le\tfrac{15}{16}$)
   and his *stated* exact constant $\tfrac12$. Since the covering radius² is
   $<1$, the Euclidean argument of Theorem 2 goes through.
4. **Theorem 2 counterexample.** Mahler's witness on p. 133 — that
   $(g|g)$, $g=\tfrac12(1{+}i_1{+}i_2{+}i_3)$, does not generate a left ideal
   — checks out exactly: the C-number
   $\{(i_1|0)[(0|i_2)(g|g)]\}\cdot\tfrac12(\bar g|{-}g)$ equals Mahler's
   stated value $(\tfrac12(-i_2-i_3)\,|\,\tfrac12(-1+i_1))$, and that value
   fails criterion (14) — it is not integral.

**Mahler's mathematics is correct.** Theorems 1 and 2 hold, and Mahler is
explicitly honest about the paper's two limitations (the non-tight constant;
the failure of Theorem 2's converse). The "explicit but incomplete"
character Petersson notes is Mahler's *own* acknowledged caveat, not a hidden
flaw.

## One finding: a typographical misprint in equation (10)

The basis (10) lists $A_8 = \bigl(\tfrac12(1{+}i_3)\ \big|\ \tfrac12(1{+}i_2)\bigr)$
(checked on the scan, zoomed). That value is **wrong**: it fails Mahler's own
criterion (14), and the eight $A_\nu$ as printed span a lattice that is *not
closed under multiplication* — so it cannot be a basis of the ring $J$. The
value forced by (14), by $J$ being a ring, and by "$J$ = Dickson's order" is
$$A_8 = \bigl(\tfrac12(1{+}i_3)\ \big|\ \tfrac12(1{+}i_3)\bigr),$$
which is exactly Dickson's basis vector $V$. With this single correction,
Mahler's basis (10) coincides with Dickson's basis (29), and everything is
consistent.

This is a **misprint, not a mathematical error**: a one-subscript slip in a
basis listing. It is harmless — Mahler's operative definition throughout the
proofs is the criterion (14), which is correct; the explicit basis (10) is
not used in the arguments.

(Minor aside: the journal *cover sheet* misprints the title as "Cayley-Di**x**on
Algebra"; the paper itself reads "Cayley-Dickson" throughout.)

## The three 1920s–40s papers, as verified

| | construction | classification / count | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dickson 1923** | $O_D$ — a genuine maximal order ✓ | Theorem XV: **3**, true is 7 — undercount | one false theorem |
| **Kirmse 1924** | $J_1$ — **not closed** ✗ | claims **8**, true is 7 — overcount | wrong construction + wrong count |

Mahler is the one of the three with no mathematical error — sound theorems,
candid about its own limits, marred only by a printer's slip in $A_8$.

## Status

Dickson (1923), Kirmse (1924), Mahler (1942): **all verified.** Coxeter (1946)
remains to be obtained — it is the source for who first stated the count
*seven* correctly, and for the precise form of the correction of Kirmse's
construction.

Facts vs. interpretation: the product identity, $J=O_D$ and its closure, the
counts 240/2160, the covering radius² $=\tfrac12$, and the Theorem-2
counterexample are **verified facts**; the diagnosis of $A_8$ as a misprint
follows necessarily — the printed value is inconsistent with Mahler's own
criterion (14) and with $J$ being a ring.
