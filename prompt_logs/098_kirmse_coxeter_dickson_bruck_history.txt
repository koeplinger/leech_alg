Prompt 098 — Historical accuracy of the Kirmse/Coxeter characterization
Date: 2026-05-17

User request (paraphrased; full text in conversation):
The characterization of the "Kirmse twist" reads sloppy ("flip two elements
in the Fano triples", "there are seven of them"). The user recalls the
correction is really "replace the unit element with one of the imaginary
units, then factor through the affected triples". User worries the paper may
contain a mistake. Asks: account of where the paper's claim came from; roles
and dates of L. E. Dickson and R. H. Bruck; was Dickson before/after Coxeter;
did Dickson wrongly claim Kirmse's integers close; did Kirmse already propose
seven correct orders and err only on an eighth; what papers exist 1920s-1940s
by Kirmse / Bruck / Dickson. User wants certainty before changing the paper.

Investigation:
  - Traced the paper's account: it derives from Petersson (2018), digested in
    prompt 037 -> evidence_and_reasoning/terminology.md and
    background_octonions.txt. The phrase "permute/swap two basis indices" is
    an AI-written gloss introduced in the prompt-037 summary; it is NOT
    Petersson's wording.
  - Read Petersson 2018 in full (lecture PDF, the project's cited source;
    Petersson read hard-copies of Kirmse 1924 and Mahler 1942).
  - Cross-checked Wikipedia "Octonion" and Baez "Integral Octonions Part 6".

Findings (sources: Petersson 2018; Baez Integral Octonions Pt 6; Wikipedia
"Octonion"; Conway-Smith 2003 via search):
  CONFIRMED:
   * The correcting operation swaps coordinate a_0 (the identity / "infinity")
     with an imaginary coordinate a_j; seven choices j=1..7 -> seven maximal
     orders. NOT "two imaginary basis indices". Matches the user's memory.
   * "Kirmse integers" = the single un-swapped set; not closed; Kirmse claimed
     (1924, without proof) it is -> "Kirmse's mistake".
   * BRUCK supplied the correction (Petersson, verbatim: Coxeter "turned to
     Bruck ... who remedied the defect by a modification of Kirmse's original
     construction"). No separate Bruck paper; it is within Coxeter 1946.
   * DICKSON (1923) independently constructed a correct integral octonion
     algebra isomorphic to Coxeter's -- 23 years before Coxeter, and before
     Kirmse's 1924 paper. Dickson did NOT repeat Kirmse's error.
   * Dates: Dickson 1923; Kirmse 1924; Mahler 1942; Coxeter 1946.
  CONTESTED / UNRESOLVED:
   * Whether Kirmse himself proposed the seven correct orders. Wikipedia says
     so ("constructed by Kirmse (1924), Dickson and Bruck"; "[Kirmse] thought
     there were eight rather than seven"); Petersson and Baez do NOT credit
     Kirmse with the seven. Not certifiable without Coxeter 1946 / Conway-Smith.
   * Whether the paper's Remark "sigma vs Fano" claim -- that Coxeter's
     correction is "not induced by a linear map on R^8" -- is correct. A swap
     a_0<->a_j IS a linear coordinate transposition, which would contradict
     that remark. Needs Coxeter 1946 to settle.
